# Semantic Bootstrap Protocol

## Objective

The bootstrap protocol is the heart of UNVTRSLR.

Its first job is not to translate an already-known language or to pre-award semantic meaning. Its first job is to establish **scoped, testable cross-system operational correspondences** from which stronger semantic interpretations may later be proposed and separately qualified.

The strongest target condition is:

- no shared vocabulary;
- no shared tokenization;
- no assumed discrete messages;
- no shared ontology;
- potentially asymmetric sensors;
- only a partially shared environment and an available interaction surface.

The protocol should degrade gracefully when more common ground exists.

## Applicability and capability rule

The numbered phases below define a rich **controlled dyadic fixture**, not one mandatory universal developmental sequence.

For every phase/test/bridge field record:

- `APPLICABLE_TESTED` when the relevant capability exists and the test is run;
- `STRUCTURALLY_INAPPLICABLE_IN_SCOPE` when independent evidence shows the capability is unavailable;
- `APPLICABILITY_UNRESOLVED` when the status is unknown.

An inapplicable test never counts as a pass. If a missing capability removes a discriminating test needed by the exact claim, the claim ceiling or coverage must fall unless a representation-neutral substitute tests the same obligation.

Persistent fields, collective systems, passive systems, one-way/asymmetric systems, fused sensor/communication substrates, and systems without stable sender/receiver roles must not be forced through dyadic symmetry by definition.

## Bootstrap loop

A dyadic active-interaction baseline is:

`observe -> detect structure -> hypothesize signalhood/function where applicable -> hypothesize correspondence -> predict -> choose discriminating interaction where available -> observe response -> update -> negotiate where possible -> test -> conserve uncertainty`

For passive, persistent, distributed, or one-way systems, unavailable operations are capability-gated rather than fabricated.

## Phase 0 — Establish an observable interaction surface

Before semantics, identify what can be observed and changed.

The controlled fixture should inventory:

- available sensory channels;
- temporal resolution;
- spatial overlap;
- controllable outputs/actions, if any;
- latency;
- noise;
- persistence;
- repeated environmental regularities;
- apparent agents or responsive systems, if any.

In an interstellar broadcast, Phase 0 is largely a physical-layer and framing problem. In an embodied probe, it includes local sensing and action. In a persistent-field or distributed system, the audit may instead bind access/influence scopes without assuming individuated agents.

## R0.5 — interaction identifiability and provenance before interpretation

`shared observable environment` is not automatically enough common ground.

Before promoting any correspondence, ask whether the candidate distinction is empirically distinguishable through the actual interaction surface.

Working lower-bound statement:

> If two candidate mappings induce the same distribution over every learner-observable interaction history under every mutually available intervention or distinguishing regime, a bootstrap restricted to that surface cannot identify which mapping is correct.

Operational rule:

`NO DISTINGUISHING OBSERVATION/REGIME -> NO IDENTIFIABLE DISTINCTION`

Candidate pre-semantic outcomes include:

- `IDENTIFIABLE_IN_SCOPE`;
- `UNDERDETERMINED_IN_SCOPE`;
- `UNIDENTIFIABLE_WITHIN_INTERACTION_SURFACE`;
- `INSUFFICIENT_INTERACTION_BUDGET`;
- `EVALUATOR_SUBSIDY_DETECTED`.

R0.5 is deliberately representation-neutral. Mandatory R0.5 state concerns identifiability, witness/provenance, preprocessing preservation, bridge attribution, scope, uncertainty, and claim ceiling. It **does not** require universal fields for form, referent, function, content, agent, speaker, listener, or semantic class.

Target-specific functional hypotheses may be carried as optional metadata when an exact claim needs them, but classification such as `reference`, `attention`, `question`, `emotion`, `grammar`, or `communicative function` belongs downstream unless independently earned.

## Phase 1 — Discover candidate structure and signalhood where applicable

The system should not assume that every observable behavior is communicative or even that a stable `signal` unit exists.

Candidate evidence that a pattern may function communicatively includes, where applicable:

- repetition under similar circumstances;
- dependence on audience presence or state;
- recipient/coupled-process response contingency;
- correction/repetition after failed response;
- turn-like alternation;
- compression or regularity exceeding surrounding behavior;
- coordination improvement when the pattern is available;
- intentional-looking modulation of a controllable channel;
- response to imitation;
- mutual adaptation over repeated encounters.

The system should keep null and lower-operational hypotheses alive, including `not communication` and `informative ecological regularity`.

### Signalhood tests

Possible tests include:

- repeat a candidate pattern and observe whether behavior changes;
- alter timing while preserving other features;
- alter form while preserving context;
- present the same form under a different context;
- withhold a previously recurrent response;
- imitate only one dimension of a multimodal behavior;
- vary apparent audience attention.

These are optional test families, not universal prerequisites. Their purpose is to identify contingent structure and function without supplying the answer label.

## Phase 2 — Establish interaction-management conventions where they emerge

Before teaching nouns, the dyadic fixture can test whether useful meta-communicative functions emerge, such as:

- attention / attend-here;
- repeat;
- same;
- different;
- acknowledge;
- reject;
- choose;
- stop;
- continue;
- query/uncertainty;
- correction.

These are **candidate bootstrap functions**, not asserted cognitive universals or mandatory R0.5 classes.

A system with repair and confirmation may learn faster than one-way labeling, but systems without those capabilities are capability-gated rather than failed by definition.

## Phase 3 — Ground stable shared regularities

Use phenomena that are simultaneously or independently observable and experimentally manipulable when possible.

Potential anchors include:

- repetition and countable recurrence;
- periodicity;
- relative duration;
- direction;
- distance;
- ordering;
- spatial coincidence;
- symmetry;
- object persistence where both sides segment objects;
- physical transformations;
- motion;
- collision;
- response to intervention;
- dimensionless ratios;
- reproducible physical phenomena.

These are candidate families, not universal ontology. For interstellar contexts, shared physics may provide stronger anchors than arbitrary human notation, but mathematics/physics must still be grounded rather than assumed already meaningful.

## Phase 4 — Infer candidate correspondences and, where applicable, referents/relations

For every recurring pattern, maintain multiple hypotheses.

Example controlled fixture:

Observed pattern `X` occurs when a red sphere is salient.

Candidate hypotheses:

- X = red;
- X = sphere;
- X = that-object;
- X = look;
- X = desirable;
- X = near-sender;
- X = topic marker;
- X = random coincidence.

The system should construct or observe regimes where those hypotheses diverge.

### Discriminating interaction example

To distinguish RED from SPHERE, when the world permits:

1. retain shape, change color;
2. retain color, change shape;
3. change both;
4. present a novel red object;
5. present a novel sphere;
6. reverse producer/receiver roles only if that capability exists and the claim makes it relevant.

The mapping is strengthened only when outcomes favor one invariant across the declared distinguishing tests.

## Phase 5 — Establish reusable relations where the system supports them

The dyadic fixture should test whether communication can express relationships such as:

- same/different;
- near/far;
- inside/outside;
- before/after;
- increase/decrease;
- source/destination;
- possession/access;
- actor/action/patient distinctions;
- conditional dependence;
- alternative/choice;
- uncertainty.

No specific relationship is assumed universal. These are experimental families.

## Phase 6 — Test combination/systematic reuse where applicable

Once several mappings exist, probe whether they combine systematically if the target system supports a meaningful combination operation.

Example controlled progression:

- red;
- sphere;
- behind;
- two;

then test a novel configuration corresponding to:

`two red spheres behind the novel object`.

Success alone is not enough; alternative shortcuts must be excluded. A system lacking separable/combinable signal components is not failed merely for lacking human-like compositionality; coverage and claim wording must reflect the inapplicability.

## Phase 7 — Role reversal as a dyadic capability-gated test

Role reversal is a strong test **when** the system has exchangeable producer/receiver roles and the exact claim predicts symmetry or re-groundability.

In that fixture, the receiver becomes sender without receiving a new dictionary, testing whether a convention is more than a one-sided response policy.

Role reversal is not a universal semantic prerequisite. Persistent fields, collective authorship, asymmetric/one-way communication, passive systems, or systems with nonexchangeable roles must use another claim-discriminating substitute where one exists. If no substitute exists for a claim that depends on productive reciprocity, the claim ceiling falls.

## Phase 8 — Counterfactual and causal testing where supported

Move beyond description when the exact claim entails causal, predictive, or counterfactual content.

Example target structure:

`if support is removed, object will fall`.

Test whether a counterpart can use the communicated structure in a novel physical arrangement.

Causal claims should remain separate from temporal correlation unless interventions or equivalent discriminating evidence support them. Systems without an intervention surface are not required to pretend one exists.

## Phase 9 — Non-equivalence discovery in the controlled mismatch fixture

Introduce cases where two access/influence scopes or representations divide the world differently.

Examples:

- one side has a single category where another has two;
- one side distinguishes a sensory dimension unavailable to the other;
- one representation emphasizes object identity while another emphasizes trajectories;
- a source convention encodes social or epistemic information the target representation lacks.

The mediator should be able to stabilize, within declared scope:

- partial mapping;
- explanatory paraphrase;
- learned new target convention;
- `UNKNOWN`;
- or `NO_FAITHFUL_EQUIVALENT_FOUND_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE`.

No finite search warrants an unqualified metaphysical `NO_FAITHFUL_EQUIVALENT` claim.

## Phase 10 — Build a durable cross-system bridge

Only after an operational mapping has survived its applicable tests should the system create a durable operational bridge entry. Semantic interpretation is a separate qualification axis.

Each bridge entry should record, as applicable:

- source pattern/channel/field or other exposure description;
- segmentation rule, if segmentation is part of the representation;
- context/scope bounds;
- operational correspondence hypothesis;
- optional semantic interpretation and its separate qualification state;
- probability/calibration;
- establishment evidence;
- counterexamples;
- applicability/coverage state;
- role-reversal result **only when applicable**;
- combination/systematic-reuse results **only when applicable**;
- target realization options;
- conservation ledger where meaningful;
- provenance distinguishing discovered/negotiated/adapted/evaluator-defined relations;
- last validation time/interaction or other currentness handle.

No missing inapplicable field counts as a pass. No inapplicable field is universally required.

## Phase 11 — Meta-communication and recursive bootstrapping

A mature dyadic or otherwise meta-communicative system may eventually communicate about the communication system itself:

- this signal is uncertain;
- repeat more slowly;
- I interpret X as Y;
- I do not understand;
- these two forms appear equivalent;
- this distinction is missing in my representation;
- I am asking rather than asserting;
- I inferred this rather than observed it.

This is the point at which a bridge can become recursively self-describing. It is a later capability, not proof that self-description was possible at zero semantics.

## Curriculum ordering

Do not assume one fixed curriculum is universal.

Candidate bootstrap order should be chosen adaptively using criteria such as:

- high observability;
- low ambiguity;
- controllability where available;
- repeatability;
- high mutual information;
- ability to generate discriminating tests;
- usefulness for later distinctions;
- low dependence on human-specific embodiment.

A physics-heavy interstellar curriculum may differ sharply from a human-human language-control curriculum.

## Active experiment selection

When multiple mappings remain plausible and active intervention is available, choose the next interaction to maximize expected discrimination while respecting safety constraints.

Conceptually:

`next_test = argmax(expected information gain over live hypotheses)`

This does not require a specific Bayesian implementation.

The system should also account for:

- cost;
- risk;
- counterpart burden;
- reversibility;
- communication latency;
- possibility of misinterpreting an intervention as hostile or meaningful.

Passive systems instead select observations, natural contrasts, or replay/playback regimes when those are available.

## Repair protocol

A robust dyadic bootstrap should exploit repair when it emerges.

Candidate repair actions include:

- repeat;
- simplify;
- isolate one variable;
- demonstrate contrast;
- request imitation;
- ask for choice between alternatives;
- revert to earlier established conventions;
- explicitly mark uncertainty;
- abandon a failed mapping.

Repair behavior should be inferred functionally rather than supplied as pre-labeled semantics.

## Stopping conditions

No operational or semantic mapping is established solely because one probability crosses an arbitrary threshold.

For the exact claim, preregister a **claim-specific evidence structure**. Candidate evidence may include:

- repeated predictive success;
- novel-instance success;
- context variation;
- intervention discrimination;
- role reversal where applicable;
- composition/systematic reuse where applicable;
- partner re-grounding where part of the claim;
- no known simpler shortcut explaining performance;
- calibration on withheld trials;
- semantic-surplus evidence when a stronger semantic interpretation is asserted.

Different claim classes require different subsets. Inapplicable axes never count as passes.

## Bootstrap failure is a valid result

The system must be able to conclude:

- no detectable communicative channel or no applicable signalhood decomposition;
- insufficient shared/overlapping interaction surface;
- structure detected but correspondence unresolved;
- operational correspondence established but semantic interpretation unresolved;
- conventions unstable;
- distinctions partially overlap only;
- interaction budget insufficient;
- partner/process appears noncooperative, deceptive, or simply outside assumed interaction model;
- physical channel too noisy;
- current representation cannot express the discovered distinction;
- `UNDERDETERMINED_IN_SCOPE`;
- `EVALUATOR_SUBSIDY_DETECTED`.

A universal mediator that cannot admit failure is a hallucination engine.

## Human verbal-language evidence refinements — Pass 1

The following refinements constrain the controlled phases above. They derive from `research/HUMAN_VERBAL_LANGUAGE.md`. Human evidence motivates responsibilities but does not prove species-independent universality.

### Self-description is a late capability, not a zero-semantics primitive

A literal self-describing protocol is circular if the receiver has no grounded way to interpret concepts such as repetition, identity, equivalence, sequence, reference, or assertion.

The stronger project hypothesis is:

`self-demonstrating -> convention-forming -> recursively self-describing`

- **self-demonstrating:** make interaction-relevant regularities observable through repetition, contrast, contingency, timing, imitation, or action consequence;
- **convention-forming:** stabilize repairable mappings that survive the applicable transfer and discriminating tests;
- **recursively self-describing:** only after enough meta-communication is grounded may the system communicate about its own forms, uncertainty, interpretations, or repair rules.

Human evidence motivates this staging but does not establish it as a universal developmental sequence.

### Joint attention and ostension are optional routes

Human word learning can occur without conventionally defined joint attention, and joint attention is not sufficient for vocabulary learning. Therefore:

- gaze, pointing, shared visual fixation, and object-centered ostension may be exploited when available;
- none may be required by the generic bootstrap interface;
- an experiment that supplies shared gaze targets or object IDs must declare that common-ground subsidy.

The weaker requirement is evidence capable of discriminating competing mappings over repeated interaction.

### Repair should be learned functionally, not pre-labeled semantically

Cross-linguistic human conversation provides strong evidence that repair is a robust interactional resource. UNVTRSLR should test repair early in human/dyadic controls, but the learner must not receive semantic labels such as `REPEAT`, `ACKNOWLEDGE`, `CLARIFY`, or `CORRECT`.

A repair-like hypothesis should earn support from observable structure such as:

1. a prior exchange appears unsuccessful or uncertain;
2. a recurrent response pattern follows;
3. one party modifies, isolates, or repeats part of the interaction;
4. subsequent coordination improves or ambiguity falls.

Repair-rich, repair-disabled, and corrupted-repair controls should be compared.

### Turn structure is evidence, not architecture canon

Human conversation exhibits strong turn-taking regularities, but an unknown communicator may be continuous, overlapping, asynchronous, distributed, or delayed.

Therefore `turn-like alternation` remains one candidate signalhood feature. Message boundaries and discrete turns must still be discoverable rather than assumed in the strongest condition.

### Iconicity is an accelerator condition

Human vocal iconicity can make novel form/meaning mappings easier to infer. Qualification should separate:

- iconic/motivated mappings;
- arbitrary mappings;
- misleading iconic cues.

Success only under human-motivated iconicity is weaker evidence than success with arbitrary convention formation.

### Discovery, invention, and adaptation require distinct provenance

A bridge must not collapse these evidence classes:

- `DISCOVERED_CORRESPONDENCE` — evidence indicates the counterpart's pre-existing behavior already tracked the candidate regularity;
- `NEGOTIATED_CONVENTION` — the mapping was created during bootstrap;
- `MIXED_OR_ADAPTED_CONVENTION` — an existing behavior was repurposed, narrowed, or extended during interaction;
- `INFERRED_EQUIVALENCE` — the mediator hypothesizes overlap between independently existing structures;
- `EVALUATOR_DEFINED_RELATION` — a test-oracle relation that must not be reported as counterpart semantics merely because the learner predicts it.

A system can be good at inventing useful conventions while being poor at decoding pre-existing communication. These are different achievements.

### Human semantic diversity raises the claim ceiling

Human languages divide semantic domains differently and may obligatorily encode distinctions that other languages express optionally or differently, including information-source/evidential distinctions.

Therefore:

- one-to-one lexical mapping is never the default assumption;
- provenance can itself be semantic content rather than merely engineering metadata;
- no-equivalence claims must be scoped to tested target representation/search families;
- human grammatical categories remain controls, not universal substrate primitives.

### Additional scoped outcomes

In addition to the generic failure vocabulary, record when applicable:

- `NEGOTIATED_BUT_NOT_DECODED`;
- `ICONICITY_DEPENDENT`;
- `REPAIR_DEPENDENT`;
- `HUMAN_PRIOR_DEPENDENT`.

## Human signed/nonverbal evidence refinements — Pass 2

These refinements derive from `research/HUMAN_NONVERBAL_AND_SIGNED.md`. They test which Pass 1 assumptions survive when speech is removed. They remain human evidence, not claims about arbitrary minds.

### Preserve overlapping and simultaneous signal structure

Do not force every candidate signal into a one-dimensional token sequence. The interaction representation should permit:

- overlapping temporal spans;
- independently moving articulators;
- synchrony as a relation distinct from adjacency;
- manual and nonmanual channels carrying different functions at the same time;
- segmentation hypotheses that can cross conventional channel boundaries.

A sequential tokenization may be one learned representation, but in the strongest condition it cannot be the only admissible ingress form.

### Channel, environment, and grammar may overlap

Signed languages can recruit physical space as a grammatical and discourse resource. Therefore the architecture should not assume a clean universal split between `message payload` and `shared environment`.

A spatial locus can simultaneously be:

- a physical location;
- an indexical cue;
- a discourse-maintained referential locus;
- part of a grammatical construction;
- or irrelevant motion.

The learner must earn that functional classification. Shared coordinates, object IDs, or viewpoint-normalized space supplied by the experiment are declared common-ground subsidies.

### Functional class remains downstream/optional, not mandatory R0.5 state

Forms such as pointing, gaze shifts, facial movements, head movements, posture changes, or repeated trajectories must not enter learner state already labeled as:

- pronoun;
- demonstrative;
- attention;
- emotion;
- question;
- negation;
- grammar;
- repair;
- signal.

The same physical family can serve linguistic, paralinguistic, affective, interactional, or noncommunicative functions depending on system and context.

R0.5 therefore records the provenance and identifiability of a distinguishing witness **without requiring `functional-class hypotheses still live` as universal state**. If an exact downstream claim concerns communicative function, reference, grammar, affect, or another class, that claim opens the relevant hypotheses and tests them there.

### Separate dyadic bootstrap from community language emergence

A bridge stabilized by two participants does not need to reproduce every property of a mature human language. Human emerging-sign evidence indicates that community use and transmission can add systematicity and grammatical complexity over time.

Track at least three human-control regimes separately:

1. `ISOLATED_OR_DYADIC_CONVENTION`;
2. `COMMUNITY_CONVENTIONALIZATION`;
3. `INTERGENERATIONAL_OR_COHORT_TRANSMISSION`.

Success at regime 1 should not be relabeled as full language emergence; failure to show mature grammar at regime 1 should not invalidate a useful operational bridge.

### Homesign is evidence for human structuring biases, not a universal grammar download

Homesign research supports the claim that humans can build structured, productive communication without access to a conventional language model. It does not establish which of those structures are necessary for semantics, nor which would appear in nonhuman cognition.

Contemporary critiques of homesign scholarship also require cautious terminology and separation between empirical structure and theoretical claims about what counts as language.

### Pointing/deixis is not self-grounding

Treat a pointing-like trajectory as an observable behavior. Candidate interpretations may include reference, participant marking, location, direction, command, attention management, or another function.

Qualification should vary:

- target presence/absence;
- competing salient loci;
- viewpoint;
- gaze alignment;
- shared coordinate frame;
- conventional versus novel pointing practice.

The system should fail if it simply maps human-looking pointing to evaluator `THAT` labels.

### Visual iconicity remains a graded condition

Compare:

- strongly motivated/iconic forms;
- weakly iconic forms;
- arbitrary conventional forms;
- misleading resemblance aligned to nuisance structure.

Iconicity can accelerate learning without settling which aspect of the form is intended as meaningful.

### Facial and body movements require cultural/contextual qualification

Do not treat canonical human facial-expression labels as universal truth. When facial or bodily patterns are used, score the counterpart's demonstrated functional use rather than resemblance to a preselected emotion taxonomy.

A facial movement can also be grammatical or prosodic in a signed language; affective and linguistic interpretations must remain separate hypotheses until evidence resolves them.

### Additional scoped outcomes

Where useful, record:

- `SIMULTANEITY_DEPENDENT` — success disappears when overlapping channels are flattened;
- `SHARED_SPATIAL_FRAME_DEPENDENT` — success relies on a supplied coordinate/viewpoint normalization;
- `POINTING_PRIOR_DEPENDENT` — performance relies on human-pretrained deictic expectations;
- `FACIAL_CATEGORY_PRIOR_DEPENDENT` — performance relies on evaluator/pretrained emotion labels rather than counterpart function;
- `COMMUNITY_TRANSMISSION_DEPENDENT` — structure appears only after multi-user or cohort transmission;
- `SEQUENTIALIZATION_LOSS` — a preprocessing step deletes or distorts meaning-bearing simultaneous structure.
