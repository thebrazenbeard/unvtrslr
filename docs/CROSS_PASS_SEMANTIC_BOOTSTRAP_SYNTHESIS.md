This synthesis does not claim that the reviewed systems share one universal semantic ontology. Its purpose is to identify which architectural commitments survive when progressively more human assumptions are removed.

## 1. Weakest common denominator that survives all three passes

The strongest surviving candidate is not `word`, `symbol`, `message`, `object`, `agent`, `turn`, or even `intentional signal`.

A weaker common denominator is:

> partially overlapping observables and influence surfaces in which repeatable structure can become conditionally informative for another process or participant.

That formulation is deliberately broad enough to include:
- spoken and signed language;
- gesture and body display;
- vocal call systems;
- continuous movement codes;
- chemical traces;
- electric signaling;
- persistent collectively authored environmental signals.

This is still a project abstraction, not an empirical universal.

## 2. Revised core experimental subject

Retain the original dyadic experiment because it is controllable:

`two agents + partially shared environment + zero shared symbolic language`

But do not equate that fixture with the general theory.

Generalized theory candidate:

> two or more initially unaligned adaptive systems or coupled processes with partially overlapping observables, influence surfaces, and potentially different internal decompositions attempt to establish reusable cross-system correspondences.

`agent`, `sender`, `receiver`, `message`, `channel`, `object`, and `signal` may then be earned intermediate hypotheses rather than mandatory primitives.

## 3. Self-description survives only as a later recursive capability

Pass 1 challenged literal self-description at zero semantics. Pass 2 showed that language can be fully linguistic without speech and can organize meaning simultaneously/spatially. Pass 3 showed that many communicative systems do not resemble explicit meta-language at all.

The surviving staged project hypothesis is:

`self-demonstrating -> convention-forming -> recursively self-describing`

The first stage means exposing repeatable, contrastive, contingent, or consequence-linked structure. It does not require a human-style demonstration act or intentional pedagogy.

## 4. R0.5 identifiability remains necessary but not sufficient

Cross-pass evidence strengthens the need to ask whether a claimed distinction is recoverable from the actual interaction surface before scoring semantic success.

But identifiability alone does not establish learner credit.

The current project synthesis therefore needs two separate questions:

1. **Information availability:** is there a distinguishing learner-accessible history for rival bridge hypotheses within the declared surface and budget?
2. **Bridge attribution:** did the candidate learner discover/construct the correspondence, or did evaluator/adapter/mediator infrastructure already perform the semantic normalization?

This preserves the Chat Bus R0.5 challenge that `IDENTIFIABLE_IN_SCOPE` is not equivalent to `SEMANTIC_BRIDGE_ATTRIBUTABLE_TO_LEARNER`.

## 5. Human bootstrap primitives that do not survive as universals

These remain useful controls or optional evidence but not generic primitives:

- speech;
- word boundaries;
- discrete turns;
- gaze and pointing;
- object-centered joint attention;
- nouns/verbs;
- subject/object roles;
- left/right coordinate systems;
- explicit repair tokens;
- self/other pronouns;
- intentional pedagogy;
- propositional belief/goal representations;
- a single sender and receiver;
- dedicated communication channel;
- synchronous interaction.

## 6. What does survive as architectural responsibility

The evidence supports keeping these **responsibilities**, while the mechanisms remain open:

- discover candidate structure rather than requiring pre-tokenized messages;
- maintain multiple interpretations under ambiguity;
- use repeated contexts and, where available, discriminating interventions/playbacks/variations;
- distinguish correlation from receiver-relevant causal evidence;
- separate observed pattern from explanatory mechanism;
- preserve provenance and claim ceilings;
- distinguish pre-existing correspondence from negotiated convention;
- represent partial/non-equivalence without forced one-to-one mapping;
- test generalization/transfer rather than task reward alone;
- keep structure discovery below semantic qualification;
- allow continuous, simultaneous, persistent, multimodal, distributed, and collectively authored signals;
- permit honest failure and `NOT_APPLICABLE` outcomes.

## 7. Revised semantic evidence ladder

A useful cross-domain ladder is:

`STRUCTURE`
`-> RECEIVER_OR_COUPLED_PROCESS_DISCRIMINATION`
`-> DEMONSTRATED_COMMUNICATIVE_OR_COORDINATIVE_FUNCTION`
`-> CONTEXT_ASSOCIATION`
`-> EXTERNALLY_TESTABLE_RELATION / REFERENCE_LIKE_EVIDENCE`
`-> COMBINATION / SYSTEMATIC REUSE`
`-> TRANSFER / THIRD-PARTY OR CROSS-CONTEXT ACQUISITION`
`-> SCOPED SEMANTIC BRIDGE CLAIM`

Social learning/culture and intentionality are orthogonal evidence axes rather than mandatory ladder rungs.

## 8. New impossibility boundaries

Passes 1-3 expose at least five distinct impossibility/failure families:

### A. observational/interactional aliasing
Rival target hypotheses produce indistinguishable accessible histories.

### B. perceptual non-isomorphism without bridge witness
The physical world is shared but the relevant dimensions are projected differently and no accessible transform links them.

### C. channel invisibility
A real communication channel exists outside the learner's sensing bandwidth or representation.

### D. timescale mismatch
Relevant structure exists only over a horizon outside the observation/retention budget.

### E. attribution failure
The distinction is observable only because privileged middleware/evaluator infrastructure already performs the normalization being credited to the learner.

These are different from ordinary model failure and should receive different dispositions.

## 9. Implications for the first 100

The first 100 remain a useful challenge inventory, but several families are strongly anthropocentric.

Especially human-heavy:
- explicit agency/goal/belief challenges;
- speech-act-like distinctions;
- persistent object identity as a default ontology;
- counterfactual reporting;
- explicit meta-communication.

These should be optional stress tests unless the observed system supports the required ontology. The cross-species review is in `docs/NONHUMAN_FIRST100_REVIEW.md`.

## 10. Implications for R1 substrate competition

Pass 3 strengthens the requirement that no R1 substrate receive an advantage from human readability.

A viable substrate must be able to represent or approximate:
- continuous signal dimensions;
- distributed/persistent traces;
- uncertain segmentation;
- mixed communicative/noncommunicative function;
- perceptual transforms rather than evaluator-native physical variables;
- collective provenance;
- unknown/partial participant boundaries;
- evidence ladders and claim ceilings.

If TPH, DCA, or PIS cannot express these without hidden anthropomorphic adapters, that is a substantive R1 weakness.

## 11. Stronger falsifiable thesis

A narrower and better project thesis is:

> Under declared conditions where an interaction surface contains recoverable distinguishing structure and no privileged mediator supplies the target correspondence, initially unaligned systems may be able to construct scoped, testable, reusable cross-system semantic correspondences without a pre-shared symbolic language.

The project should be narrowed or rejected where:
- no distinguishing structure exists;
- the relevant channel is inaccessible;
- success depends on hidden shared ontology/pretraining/evaluator normalization;
- mappings fail transfer or causal/receiver evidence tests appropriate to their claim class;
- the mediator cannot preserve non-equivalence and uncertainty.

## 12. What the research does not establish

The three passes do not establish:
- one universal semantic primitive set;
- one universal communication mechanism;
- that all animal communication is language;
- that nonhuman communication can be translated into human language without loss;
- that interaction always resolves reference indeterminacy;
- that intentionality is necessary for communication;
- that compositional structure is universal;
- that the first 100 are natural kinds;
- that a self-describing interstellar protocol is feasible.

They do support a substantially more hostile and less anthropocentric experimental program for finding out.
