**Goal:** determine which semantic-bootstrap responsibilities survive removal of acoustic speech by studying full signed languages, homesign/emerging signed systems, silent gesture, and nonlinguistic human communication without collapsing those categories.

## Task 1 — Lock category boundaries and evidence review

Create `research/HUMAN_NONVERBAL_AND_SIGNED.md`.

Required categories:

1. established signed languages — full linguistic systems, not merely nonverbal behavior;
2. emerging signed languages / homesign — language emergence under altered access/community conditions;
3. silent gesture/pantomime — ad hoc or experimentally conventionalizing systems;
4. nonlinguistic human signals — pointing/gaze, facial/body expression, touch/proxemics, timing and other modalities where evidence is adequate.

For each finding record: observed evidence, claim ceiling, bootstrap implication, hidden human prior, and false-success risk.

## Task 3 — Pressure the bootstrap protocol

Modify `docs/BOOTSTRAP_PROTOCOL.md`.

Required challenges:
- discrete sequential tokens are not universal;
- multiple articulators can encode information simultaneously;
- physical space can be grammaticalized but shared spatial grammar must not be preloaded;
- iconicity is modality-dependent and can coexist with conventional grammar;
- community transmission changes what structures emerge;
- pointing can straddle linguistic and gestural function, so `deixis` cannot be identified from form alone;
- facial/body cues may be linguistic, paralinguistic, affective, or noncommunicative depending on system/context;
- repair/meta-communication may be multimodal and distributed across channels.
