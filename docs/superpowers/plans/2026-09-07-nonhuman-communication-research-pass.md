**Goal:** challenge UNVTRSLR with empirically established nonhuman communication across vocal, gestural, visual, tactile, chemical, electrical, and environmental-modification channels without relabeling nonhuman systems as human-like language.

## Evidence discipline

Use `vocal` / `non-vocal`, not `verbal` / `nonverbal`, unless discussing a source's own terminology.

For every animal system distinguish:

- signal structure;
- receiver discrimination;
- demonstrated communicative function;
- environmental/social context association;
- reference-like evidence;
- sequence/combinatorial evidence;
- social learning;
- turn-taking/repair-like behavior;
- intentionality evidence where experimentally supported;
- semantic evidence actually demonstrated;
- major alternative explanations and unknowns.

Acoustic classification, sequence prediction, synthesis, individual recognition, or mutual information alone never qualifies as semantic translation.

## Task 1 — Vocal/acoustic systems

Research and integrate representative systems chosen for distinct evidence pressures:

- nonhuman primates: alarm/call context, combinatorial sequences, vocal turn exchange;
- cetaceans: dolphin signature whistles and sperm-whale coda structure/context;
- birds: vocal learning and experimentally tested combinatorial call structure;
- elephants: individually specific/name-like vocal addressing where supported;
- bats or other mammals where vocal identity/context evidence adds a distinct control.

Prefer playback/intervention evidence over corpus-only association when available.

## Task 2 — Non-vocal systems

Research and integrate:

- honeybee waggle dance as continuous spatial-vector communication;
- great-ape gestures as flexible, audience-sensitive intentional signaling evidence;
- visual/body displays where function is experimentally or ethologically well-supported;
- chemical communication as a channel family that attacks discrete-message and shared-timescale assumptions;
- electrical/substrate-borne/tactile communication where it provides a genuinely different signaling substrate;
- environmental modification only where evidence supports communicative use rather than mere world alteration.

## Task 4 — Update source notes and claims ledger

Create a Pass 3 source supplement and extend `research/CLAIMS_AND_EVIDENCE.md` with nonhuman-specific claim ceilings.

Key expected challenges:

- communication can be continuous, spatial, chemical, electrical, multimodal, or temporally persistent;
- signal meaning may depend on receiver ecology and perceptual model;
- individual identity/addressing is not the same as names in the human linguistic sense;
- combinatorial call structure is not automatically syntax/semantics;
- context association is not referential meaning without stronger controls;
- intentionality evidence varies strongly by system and should not be required universally;
- population/culture/social learning may shape signals;
- animal data are usually observationally sparse relative to human-language ground truth.

## Task 6 — Cross-pass synthesis

Update project thesis/research landscape only after the evidence matrix is stable.

Required synthesis questions:

1. What is the weakest common denominator across human and nonhuman communication evidence?
2. Which apparently useful human bootstrap primitives disappear cross-species?
3. Does `two agents + shared observable environment` remain the correct problem statement, or should the general theory speak about coupled adaptive processes / partially overlapping observability?
4. Which first-100 challenges are strongly anthropocentric and should be optional or explicitly human-control-only?
5. What new impossibility/identifiability conditions emerge from radically different sensing and communication channels?
