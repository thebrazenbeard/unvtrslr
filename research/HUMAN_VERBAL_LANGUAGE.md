# Human Verbal-Language Evidence for Semantic Bootstrap

Status: `PASS_1_EVIDENCE_REVIEW / HUMAN_SCOPE_ONLY`

Bound project source at pass start: `main@0b3285393ff319f18f40af8c2ba863edea0df2bf`

This review asks what empirical work on human spoken/verbal language actually contributes to UNVTRSLR's harder target: two agents with no shared symbolic language attempting to establish grounded, auditable semantic distinctions through a shared interaction surface.

The central discipline is negative as much as positive: **human-language evidence may motivate bootstrap responsibilities without establishing species-independent primitives.**

Evidence labels used below:

- `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK` — directly supported by cited empirical/review literature;
- `SUPPORTED_LIMITED_SCOPE` — supported under materially narrower human conditions;
- `PROJECT_HYPOTHESIS` — UNVTRSLR proposal motivated but not validated by the literature;
- `INFERENCE` — synthesis from multiple findings;
- `CHALLENGE` — adversarial implication or false-success risk;
- `UNKNOWN` — evidence does not settle the question.

## 1. Cross-situational learning: ambiguity can be reduced across encounters

Smith & Yu (2008) showed that 12- and 14-month-old infants can aggregate statistical regularities across individually ambiguous word-scene pairings and recover word-referent mappings.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK / HUMAN DEVELOPMENT`

**What this supports:** a semantic bootstrap should maintain multiple live mapping hypotheses and accumulate evidence across repeated contexts rather than require one-shot ostension.

**What it does not support:** that passive cross-situational statistics are sufficient for arbitrary semantic grounding, unknown signalhood, asymmetric ontology, or radically nonhuman agents. The study supplies words, scenes, human infants, and a learning context already rich with human perceptual and social priors.

**UNVTRSLR consequence:** repeated ambiguous co-occurrence is legitimate evidence, but not a qualification gate by itself. Interventions, transfer, role reversal, and shortcut controls remain necessary.

Reference: Smith, L. & Yu, C. (2008). *Infants rapidly learn word-referent mappings via cross-situational statistics*. Cognition 106(3), 1558-1568. DOI `10.1016/j.cognition.2007.06.010`.

## 2. Joint attention: powerful human infrastructure, not a mandatory universal primitive

Akhtar & Gernsbacher (2007) review evidence that word learning can occur without conventionally defined joint attention, while joint attention can occur without commensurate vocabulary learning. They also note that standard measures rely heavily on overt visual indicators and vary culturally.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK / REVIEW`

**What this supports:** gaze following, pointing, shared visual attention, and human-style ostension are useful experimental conditions but should not be mandatory bootstrap primitives.

**What it does not support:** that no coordination of attention or evidence selection is needed. A weaker requirement may still exist: the agents need some recoverable evidence that lets them discriminate candidate correspondences.

**UNVTRSLR consequence:** R0.5 should test whether a usable cross-party evidence relation exists without requiring gaze, pointing, object-centered attention, or a shared visual field.

Reference: Akhtar, N. & Gernsbacher, M. A. (2007). *Joint Attention and Vocabulary Development: A Critical Look*. Language and Linguistics Compass 1(3), 195-207. DOI `10.1111/j.1749-818X.2007.00014.x`.

## 3. Turn-taking: interaction has timing structure before semantic agreement is complete

Stivers et al. (2009) compared ordinary conversation in ten languages and found strong commonalities in the avoidance of overlap and minimization of silence, alongside quantitative cultural variation in response timing.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK / CROSS-LINGUISTIC HUMAN CONVERSATION`

**What this supports:** interaction management can carry useful structure independent of lexical content. Timing, alternation, response contingency, and initiation/response organization are legitimate candidate cues for signalhood and negotiation.

**Claim ceiling:** these are regularities of human conversation, not proof that arbitrary communicators organize interaction into discrete turns.

**UNVTRSLR consequence:** turn-like alternation should be detectable evidence, never a required channel assumption. Continuous, overlapping, asynchronous, swarm, and delayed-response controls must remain possible.

Reference: Stivers, T. et al. (2009). *Universals and cultural variation in turn-taking in conversation*. PNAS 106(26), 10587-10592. DOI `10.1073/pnas.0903616106`.

## 4. Repair: communication systems become much more learnable when misunderstanding itself is observable

Dingemanse et al. (2015) compared other-initiated repair across twelve languages from five continents and found a robust shared organization for identifying and resolving communication problems.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK / CROSS-LINGUISTIC HUMAN CONVERSATION`

**What this supports:** repair, repetition, clarification, and correction are strong early functional targets because they let participants expose uncertainty and discriminate competing interpretations.

**Critical distinction:** the learner must not receive evaluator labels such as `REPAIR`, `ACKNOWLEDGE`, or `CORRECTION`. A repair-like function has to be inferred from observable contingencies: a prior exchange fails, a pattern is produced, the counterpart modifies/repeats behavior, and coordination subsequently improves.

**UNVTRSLR consequence:** add repair-rich and repair-disabled/corrupted controls. Measure whether repair accelerates grounding without making it a universal prerequisite.

Reference: Dingemanse, M. et al. (2015). *Universal Principles in the Repair of Communication Problems*. PLOS ONE 10(9):e0136100. DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0136100`.

## 5. Humans can invent conventions under severe channel restrictions

Galantucci (2005) placed human pairs in a cooperative task where they were physically separated and could exchange only graphic signals through a medium that prevented ordinary standard symbols. Novel communication systems emerged rapidly and relied on both explicit signs and task/context structure.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK / EXPERIMENTAL SEMIOTICS`

**What this supports:** negotiated convention formation from initially nonconventional signals is empirically real for humans.

**What this does not support:** recovery of a counterpart's pre-existing language or semantics. The experiment demonstrates **invention/stabilization**, not necessarily **discovery/translation**.

**UNVTRSLR consequence:** semantic provenance must distinguish at least:

- `DISCOVERED_CORRESPONDENCE`;
- `NEGOTIATED_CONVENTION`;
- `MIXED_OR_ADAPTED_CONVENTION`;
- `INFERRED_EQUIVALENCE`;
- `EVALUATOR_DEFINED_RELATION`.

Conflating these achievements would let a system that is excellent at inventing a task code masquerade as a translator.

Reference: Galantucci, B. (2005). *An Experimental Study of the Emergence of Human Communication Systems*. Cognitive Science 29(5), 737-767. DOI `10.1207/s15516709cog0000_34`.

## 6. Iconicity can reduce the arbitrariness burden, but it is not a universal seed language

Perlman & Lupyan (2018) showed that people can create novel iconic vocalizations whose meanings naïve listeners identify above chance across multiple semantic domains. Ćwiek et al. (2021) extended this with a broad cross-cultural test showing that novel vocalizations can be understood above chance across diverse language backgrounds.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK / HUMAN VOCAL ICONICITY`

**What this supports:** form-meaning resemblance can bootstrap some human correspondences without a pre-existing conventional lexicon.

**Claim ceiling:** human vocal iconicity depends on shared bodies, perception, action experience, acoustic affordances, and human similarity judgments. It cannot be assumed available across radically different sensors or cognition.

**UNVTRSLR consequence:** iconicity is a **condition and accelerator**, not a universal answer key. Qualification should include paired conditions:

- motivated/iconic mapping available;
- arbitrary mapping required;
- false iconic cue correlated with the wrong factor.

A system that only succeeds when human-motivated resemblance exists has not demonstrated the strongest bootstrap thesis.

References:

- Perlman, M. & Lupyan, G. (2018). *People Can Create Iconic Vocalizations to Communicate Various Meanings to Naïve Listeners*. Scientific Reports 8:2634. DOI `10.1038/s41598-018-20961-6`.
- Ćwiek, A. et al. (2021). *Novel vocalizations are understood across cultures*. Scientific Reports 11:10108. DOI `10.1038/s41598-021-89445-4`.

## 7. Structure can emerge through cultural transmission after a signaling system exists

Kirby, Cornish & Smith (2008) used iterated artificial-language transmission and showed that repeated cultural transmission can produce increasingly learnable and structured systems; compositional structure emerges under interacting pressures for learnability and expressivity.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK / HUMAN ITERATED LEARNING`

**What this supports:** UNVTRSLR should distinguish three temporal problems:

1. **initial grounding** — establish first usable correspondences;
2. **convention stabilization** — make them repairable and reusable across partners/contexts;
3. **systematization** — repeated use/transmission may create more regular or compositional structure.

**Claim ceiling:** the experiments prespecify meanings, signal sets, learners, and transmission regimes. They do not demonstrate compositionality emerging from zero shared channel or ontology.

Reference: Kirby, S., Cornish, H. & Smith, K. (2008). *Cumulative cultural evolution in the laboratory: An experimental approach to the origins of structure in human language*. PNAS 105(31), 10681-10686. DOI `10.1073/pnas.0707835105`.

## 8. Human language diversity is a direct warning against turning familiar grammar into bootstrap canon

Evans & Levinson (2009) argue from typological evidence that human languages show deep diversity across phonology, morphology, syntax, lexicon, and meaning and that strong direct structural universals are much rarer than often assumed. Their target article is part of an active theoretical debate, so UNVTRSLR should use it conservatively: as evidence that proposed universals require explicit empirical burden, not as proof that no human language universals exist.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK / CONTESTED INTERPRETIVE FRAME`

**UNVTRSLR consequence:** categories such as noun, verb, grammatical subject, tense, recursive phrase structure, or a particular word order cannot enter the universal bootstrap interface merely because they are familiar in major languages.

Reference: Evans, N. & Levinson, S. C. (2009). *The myth of language universals: Language diversity and its importance for cognitive science*. Behavioral and Brain Sciences 32(5), 429-448. DOI `10.1017/S0140525X0999094X`.

## 9. Semantic typology: languages partition shared domains differently

Kemp, Xu & Regier (2018) review cross-linguistic evidence from domains including color, kinship, number, folk biology, and spatial relations showing that languages carve the world into named categories differently, while variation is constrained rather than arbitrary.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK / REVIEW`

**What this supports:** translation should expect partial overlap, one-to-many/many-to-one mappings, and category boundary mismatch even among humans.

**UNVTRSLR consequence:** evaluator categories must not automatically be treated as the target's natural ontology. Semantic conservation should be evaluated over tested distinctions and contexts rather than one-to-one word equivalence.

Reference: Kemp, C., Xu, Y. & Regier, T. (2018). *Semantic Typology and Efficient Communication*. Annual Review of Linguistics 4, 109-128. DOI `10.1146/annurev-linguistics-011817-045406`.

## 10. Evidentiality shows that languages can obligate different provenance distinctions

Aikhenvald's typological work and later reviews document languages with grammaticalized systems for indicating information source, including distinctions such as direct observation, report, inference, or other evidence classes. English can express source information but generally does not obligatorily grammaticalize it in the same way.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK / HUMAN LINGUISTIC TYPOLOGY`

**What this supports:** two human languages may differ not only in vocabulary but in which semantic/provenance distinctions are structurally obligatory or routinely encoded.

**UNVTRSLR consequence:** provenance is not just an engineering audit field. It can itself be semantically relevant. The mediator must preserve the difference between `observed`, `reported`, `inferred`, and `unknown` even when the target renderer lexicalizes or grammaticalizes those distinctions differently.

References:

- Aikhenvald, A. Y. (2004). *Evidentiality*. Oxford University Press. DOI `10.1093/oso/9780199263882.001.0001`.
- Murray, S. E. (2021). *Evidentiality, Modality, and Speech Acts*. Annual Review of Linguistics 7, 213-233. DOI `10.1146/annurev-linguistics-011718-012625`.

## 11. Natural Semantic Metalanguage is a serious human hypothesis baseline, not project canon

NSM research proposes a finite set of semantic primes intended to recur across human languages; contemporary formulations commonly identify 65 primes. This is one of the strongest explicit empirical hypotheses about a cross-linguistic human semantic core.

**Evidence class:** `SUPPORTED_PRIOR_WORK AS A RESEARCH PROGRAM / UNIVERSALITY CLAIM CONTESTED`

**UNVTRSLR use:** benchmark whether grounded agents independently recover distinctions resembling proposed primes under human-language controls.

**UNVTRSLR prohibition:** do not preload the primes into R0.5/R1 as universal ontology, and do not generalize a human-language claim to nonhuman or extraterrestrial cognition.

Reference: Wierzbicka, A. (2021). *Semantic Primitives, fifty years later*. Russian Journal of Linguistics 25(2). See existing project bibliography.

## 12. Implications for the `first 100`

Human evidence does **not** justify a fixed universal first-100 vocabulary or a single developmental order.

What it does support is a **dependency-aware challenge system**:

- some mappings become easier once repair or contrastive interaction exists;
- repeated contexts can accumulate evidence without perfect shared attention;
- motivated/iconic forms can accelerate some mappings but must not be required;
- semantic category boundaries can differ across languages;
- provenance distinctions may themselves be semantically encoded;
- systematization/composition may strengthen after conventions already exist.

Therefore the existing 100 evaluator labels should remain challenge descriptions, while prerequisites become explicit hypotheses with alternative paths.

## 13. Strongest human-language challenge to the UNVTRSLR thesis

The best human evidence begins from enormous hidden common ground: broadly similar bodies, developmental trajectories, perceptual systems, social cognition, ecological experience, biological constraints, and often extensive cultural scaffolding.

So the correct inference is not:

> humans can bootstrap conventions, therefore arbitrary intelligences can bootstrap semantics.

It is:

> humans demonstrate several mechanisms that can reduce semantic uncertainty under particular shared priors; UNVTRSLR must identify which of those mechanisms survive when those priors are removed.

That makes human language a **graded control family**, not the proof case.

## 14. R1/R2 pressure from this pass

### R0.5

Add an identifiability/common-ground audit before substrate competition. A target distinction should not be judged learnable if no mutually available observation/intervention could distinguish candidate mappings.

### R1

Do not add human grammatical categories to the common substrate interface because they appear useful in linguistic description. Richer structure must still earn itself against DCA/PIS or leaner rivals.

### R2

Add or preserve negative controls for:

- hidden shared attention cues;
- shared object segmentation;
- experimenter/task scaffolding mistaken for discovered semantics;
- negotiated task code reported as decoded pre-existing language;
- human iconicity shortcuts;
- repair labels supplied by the evaluator;
- pretrained world/language priors;
- evaluator category recovery without counterpart evidence;
- co-trained conventions that fail third-party acquisition.

## 15. Current claim ceiling after the human verbal pass

Human verbal-language research materially supports the plausibility of **ambiguity reduction, convention formation, interaction repair, context-sensitive mapping, and later systematization** under human conditions.

It does **not** establish:

- a universal semantic vocabulary;
- a universal curriculum;
- universal discrete turns/messages;
- necessary joint attention;
- species-neutral iconicity;
- unique semantic identifiability;
- guaranteed grounding from a shared physical world;
- a general self-describing protocol from zero semantics;
- or feasibility of the full interstellar target.

Those remain project hypotheses or open questions.
