# Nonhuman Communication Evidence for Semantic Bootstrap

Status: `PASS_3_EVIDENCE_REVIEW / CROSS_SPECIES_SCOPE`

Predecessor checkpoint: `HUMAN_NONVERBAL_SIGNED_PASS_2@390e533abc85682177327af6bbfa56c9719a0019`

This pass deliberately uses **vocal** and **non-vocal**, not `verbal` and `nonverbal`. Calling a nonhuman vocal system `verbal language` would prejudge exactly what UNVTRSLR is trying to discover.

The objective is not to rank species by how human-like they are. It is to identify communication mechanisms that attack hidden assumptions in a zero-shared-symbol semantic bootstrap.

## Evidence ladder

For each system, keep these claims separate:

1. `STRUCTURE` — a measurable signal pattern exists;
2. `RECEIVER_DISCRIMINATION` — receivers distinguish signal variants;
3. `COMMUNICATIVE_FUNCTION` — signal production/reception affects a social or ecological interaction in a demonstrated way;
4. `CONTEXT_ASSOCIATION` — signal variant covaries with environmental/social context;
5. `REFERENCE_LIKE_EVIDENCE` — playback/intervention supports information about an external entity/event beyond immediate arousal;
6. `COMBINATION` — units/features combine systematically;
7. `SOCIAL_LEARNING/CULTURE` — signal form/use is socially transmitted or community-specific;
8. `INTENTIONALITY_EVIDENCE` — production changes with audience/goal/response in ways meeting operational criteria;
9. `SEMANTIC_CLAIM_CEILING` — strongest claim the evidence can carry.

No species must climb this ladder in order, and some rungs may be inappropriate for some communication systems.

# Part I — Vocal / acoustic systems

## 1. Vervet monkey alarm calls: playback can establish more than correlation, but the lexical analogy is still bounded

Classic field/playback work by Seyfarth, Cheney & Marler (1980) found acoustically different alarm calls associated with major predator classes and different adaptive receiver responses. Playback in the absence of the predator elicited the corresponding responses, which is substantially stronger than observing call/context correlation alone.

Later quantitative and contextual work complicates a simple `one call = one predator word` story. Vervet calls overlap acoustically across contexts and receiver interpretation depends on more than a dictionary analogy.

- `STRUCTURE`: strong.
- `RECEIVER_DISCRIMINATION`: strong playback evidence.
- `COMMUNICATIVE_FUNCTION`: strong alarm function.
- `REFERENCE_LIKE_EVIDENCE`: strong by animal-communication standards; often called functionally referential/semantic in the literature.
- `INTENTIONALITY_EVIDENCE`: not established by the classic playback result.
- `SEMANTIC_CLAIM_CEILING`: predator-linked alarm categories with receiver-relevant external information; **not** evidence of human-like lexical semantics or a general word system.

UNVTRSLR consequence: a context classifier is not enough. A stronger animal semantic control should include signal playback with the external stimulus absent, context swaps, matched arousal controls, and downstream behavioral predictions.

References:
- Seyfarth, R. M., Cheney, D. L. & Marler, P. (1980). *Vervet monkey alarm calls: Semantic communication in a free-ranging primate*. Animal Behaviour 28(4):1070-1094. DOI `10.1016/S0003-3472(80)80097-2`.
- Price, T. et al. (2015). *Vervets revisited: A quantitative analysis of alarm call structure and context specificity*. Scientific Reports 5:13220. DOI `10.1038/srep13220`.

## 2. Campbell's monkey combinations: compositional-like effects deserve a control, not a human syntax label

Campbell's monkeys produce alarm-call forms such as `krak`, `hok`, and suffixed variants with `-oo`; observations and playback/cross-species response work support predictable changes in receiver response when the suffix is present. Call sequences also show nonrandom context-linked organization.

The animal communication literature treats this as one of the strongest candidates for compositional syntax outside humans, while reviews also emphasize unresolved questions about exact production meaning and the human morphological analogy.

- `STRUCTURE`: strong discrete call and sequence structure.
- `RECEIVER_DISCRIMINATION`: playback evidence supports sensitivity to suffixed versus unsuffixed forms.
- `COMBINATION`: strong candidate for meaning-modifying combination.
- `SEMANTIC_CLAIM_CEILING`: systematic composition-like modification of alarm information; not proof of human-like morphology/syntax.

UNVTRSLR consequence: distinguish **distributional combination** from **compositional meaning**. A valid control should compare natural and experimentally recombined forms and test whether receiver behavior tracks the predicted combined function on held-out combinations.

References:
- Ouattara, K., Lemasson, A. & Zuberbühler, K. (2009/2010). Campbell's monkey call-combination work; see PNAS article *Campbell's monkeys concatenate vocalizations into context-specific call sequences* and related suffix studies.
- Suzuki, T. N., Wheatcroft, D. & Griesser, M. (2020). *The syntax-semantics interface in animal vocal communication*. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B 375:20180405. DOI `10.1098/rstb.2018.0405`.

## 3. Marmoset vocal turn exchange: interactive timing exists, but even the mechanism can be overfit

Marmosets engage in extended antiphonal vocal exchanges and actively avoid overlap. Developmental work shows parental contingent calling affects infant vocal acoustics. Earlier work modeled exchanges as coupled oscillators; a larger 2024 dataset replicated overlap avoidance but did **not** replicate the coupled-oscillator mechanism, instead supporting a features-based model incorporating internal and social-context factors.

- `STRUCTURE`: strong reciprocal timing organization.
- `COMMUNICATIVE_FUNCTION`: antiphonal contact communication.
- `SOCIAL DEVELOPMENT`: contingent parental interaction shapes aspects of vocal development.
- `SEMANTIC_CLAIM_CEILING`: interactive timing/turn-management evidence, not evidence that the calls form a propositional language.

UNVTRSLR consequence: even when a high-level behavioral regularity is real, the explanatory mechanism can be wrong. The bootstrap should store `observed turn-like contingency` separately from `coupled-oscillator explanation` or any other model.

References:
- Takahashi, D. Y., Narayanan, D. Z. & Ghazanfar, A. A. (2013). *Coupled Oscillator Dynamics of Vocal Turn-Taking in Monkeys*. Current Biology 23:2162-2168. DOI `10.1016/j.cub.2013.09.005`.
- Takahashi, D. Y. et al. (2016). *Early development of turn-taking with parents shapes vocal acoustics in infant marmoset monkeys*. Philosophical Transactions B 371:20150370. DOI `10.1098/rstb.2015.0370`.
- Miller, C. T. et al. (2024). *A model of marmoset monkey vocal turn-taking*. Philosophical Transactions B / Royal Society publication; larger-sample analysis rejects a general coupled-oscillator account while preserving turn-taking behavior.

## 4. Bottlenose dolphin signature whistles: learned individual labels are unusually strong evidence, but `name` remains an analogy

Bottlenose dolphins develop individually distinctive signature whistles. King & Janik (2013) used synthesized signature-whistle playbacks and showed that dolphins responded when a close associate's whistle contour was copied, supporting the use of learned vocal labels to address specific individuals.

- `STRUCTURE`: individually distinctive learned whistle contours.
- `RECEIVER_DISCRIMINATION`: strong playback evidence.
- `ADDRESSING/REFERENCE_LIKE`: unusually strong individual-identity evidence.
- `SOCIAL LEARNING`: vocal production learning is central.
- `SEMANTIC_CLAIM_CEILING`: learned vocal labels for individual identity/addressing; `name` is a useful analogy, not evidence that dolphin identity concepts equal human proper names.

UNVTRSLR consequence: distinguish `signal correlated with emitter identity`, `receiver recognizes caller`, `signal copies addressee label`, and `arbitrary address label`. They are different hypotheses and require different interventions.

Reference: King, S. L. & Janik, V. M. (2013). *Bottlenose dolphins can use learned vocal labels to address each other*. PNAS 110(32):13216-13221. DOI `10.1073/pnas.1304459110`.

## 5. Sperm whale codas: rich combinatorial acoustic structure is not yet translated semantics

Sperm whales exchange rhythmic click sequences called codas. Gero, Whitehead & Rendell (2016) found identity cues at individual, social-unit, and vocal-clan levels; vocal-clan repertoires are culturally structured. Sharma et al. (2024) showed context-sensitive and combinatorial organization involving rhythm, tempo, rubato, and ornamentation.

Critically, the 2024 paper states that its analysis concerns **structure, not semantics**, and that communicative functions for many codas remain unknown; it calls for interactive playback to test semantic hypotheses.

- `STRUCTURE`: very strong, multi-timescale and combinatorial.
- `IDENTITY/CULTURE`: strong clan/unit/individual cue evidence.
- `CONVERSATIONAL CONTEXT`: strong contextual modulation/coordination evidence.
- `SEMANTICS`: largely unresolved for most coda features.
- `SEMANTIC_CLAIM_CEILING`: structured culturally transmitted communication with large information-carrying capacity; **not a translated whale language**.

UNVTRSLR consequence: acoustic tokenization, prediction, clustering, synthesis, embeddings, or `phonetic alphabet` recovery belong below the semantic acceptance gate. The project should be able to produce a high-confidence structure model while returning `SEMANTICS_UNRESOLVED`.

References:
- Gero, S., Whitehead, H. & Rendell, L. (2016). *Individual, unit and vocal clan level identity cues in sperm whale codas*. Royal Society Open Science 3:150372. DOI `10.1098/rsos.150372`.
- Sharma, P. et al. (2024). *Contextual and combinatorial structure in sperm whale vocalisations*. Nature Communications 15:3617. DOI `10.1038/s41467-024-47221-8`.

## 6. Japanese great tits: playback supports composition-like interpretation of call sequences

Suzuki, Wheatcroft & Griesser (2016) experimentally tested Japanese great-tit call combinations. Birds responded differently to ordered combinations, and sequence order mattered, providing unusually strong evidence that receivers extract combined information from call components rather than merely responding to total acoustic energy or co-occurrence.

- `STRUCTURE`: discrete call components and ordered sequences.
- `RECEIVER_DISCRIMINATION`: playback intervention.
- `COMPOSITION`: strong candidate for compositional syntax by comparative standards.
- `SEMANTIC_CLAIM_CEILING`: experimentally supported composition-like receiver interpretation for a bounded call system, not open-ended human syntax.

UNVTRSLR consequence: composition qualification requires destructive controls: reverse order, shuffle components, substitute matched unfamiliar notes, and test predicted receiver behavior rather than only classifier accuracy.

Reference: Suzuki, T. N., Wheatcroft, D. & Griesser, M. (2016). *Experimental evidence for compositional syntax in bird calls*. Nature Communications 7:10986. DOI `10.1038/ncomms10986`.

## 7. Parrot vocal imitation: individual addressing can arise through copying rather than arbitrary labels

Orange-fronted conures can imitate contact calls. Playback experiments found stronger/faster responses from the individual whose call was imitated, supporting flexible addressing of specific flock members.

- `VOCAL LEARNING`: strong.
- `ADDRESSING`: playback-supported.
- `MECHANISM`: receiver-directed imitation/copying rather than evidence for an arbitrary independent label.
- `SEMANTIC_CLAIM_CEILING`: individual-specific addressing, not human naming syntax.

UNVTRSLR consequence: the same behavioral function — addressing an individual — can be implemented through materially different representational mechanisms. Do not canonize `name token` as the semantic primitive.

Reference: Balsby, T. J. S., Momberg, J. V. & Dabelsteen, T. (2012). *Vocal Imitation in Parrots Allows Addressing of Specific Individuals in a Dynamic Communication Network*. PLOS ONE 7:e49747. DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0049747`.

## 8. African elephants: individually specific name-like calls, with playback evidence and a careful ceiling

Pardo et al. (2024) combined machine learning with field playback and reported receiver-specific information in African savannah elephant calls. Elephants responded more strongly to calls originally addressed to them. The receiver-specific feature does not appear to be simple imitation of the receiver's own calls, motivating the authors' `name-like` description.

- `STRUCTURE`: receiver-specific acoustic information.
- `RECEIVER_DISCRIMINATION`: field playback evidence.
- `ADDRESSING`: strong evidence.
- `ARBITRARINESS`: evidence argues against simple imitation, but complete encoding mechanism remains unresolved.
- `SEMANTIC_CLAIM_CEILING`: individually specific, learned/name-like addressing; not proof of human proper-name semantics or a larger elephant lexicon.

UNVTRSLR consequence: `name-like` must remain an evaluator analogy with explicit operational evidence underneath it.

Reference: Pardo, M. A. et al. (2024). *African elephants address one another with individually specific name-like calls*. Nature Ecology & Evolution. DOI `10.1038/s41559-024-02420-w`.

## 9. Egyptian fruit bats: high information content is not the same thing as decoded meaning

Long-duration audio/video monitoring of Egyptian fruit bats found that vocalizations statistically contain information about caller identity, addressee, interaction context, likely behavioral response, and other variables.

- `STRUCTURE/INFORMATION`: strong.
- `DIRECTEDNESS`: many calls were pairwise/directed.
- `CONTEXT ASSOCIATION`: strong.
- `SEMANTIC CLAIM`: limited because classifier-readable information and behavioral covariance do not by themselves establish what a receiver represents or intends.

UNVTRSLR consequence: `classifier predicts addressee/context` is an intermediate evidence class, not translation. The evaluator should require playback/manipulation or other causal evidence before promoting a semantic interpretation.

Reference: Prat, Y., Taub, M. & Yovel, Y. (2016). *Everyday bat vocalizations contain information about emitter, addressee, context, and behavior*. Scientific Reports 6:39419. DOI `10.1038/srep39419`.

# Part II — Non-vocal systems

## 10. Great-ape gestures: intentionality evidence is stronger in gesture than in many animal vocal systems

Great ape gestural research reports audience targeting, sensitivity to recipient attention, response waiting, persistence, and elaboration when an attempted communication does not achieve its apparent goal. Wild chimpanzee studies have mapped recurrent gesture forms to bounded social goals using behavioral outcome criteria.

- `STRUCTURE`: large gesture repertoires.
- `AUDIENCE DIRECTEDNESS`: strong.
- `PERSISTENCE/REPAIR-LIKE`: strong operational evidence.
- `INTENTIONALITY`: among the strongest nonhuman cases under behavioral criteria, while mental-state claims still require caution.
- `SEMANTIC CLAIM_CEILING`: flexible goal-directed gestural communication with recurrent intended outcomes; not human propositional language.

UNVTRSLR consequence: intentionality cannot be a universal prerequisite because many established communication systems may operate without evidence of flexible intentional production. But when audience checking/persistence exists, it is powerful evidence for separating communication from incidental behavior.

References:
- Hobaiter, C. & Byrne, R. W. (2014). *The Meanings of Chimpanzee Gestures*. Current Biology 24(14):1596-1600. DOI `10.1016/j.cub.2014.05.066`.
- Byrne, R. W. et al. (2017). *Great ape gestures: intentional communication with a rich set of innate signals*. Animal Cognition 20:755-769. DOI `10.1007/s10071-017-1096-4`.

## 11. Honeybee waggle dance: a continuous embodied code grounded in the bee's perceptual metric

Honeybee waggle dances recruit nestmates to resources. Dance orientation and timing encode route information. Esch et al. (2001) showed that the distance component tracks optic-flow experience rather than an observer-independent absolute distance; recruited bees act on the dance-derived information. Riley et al. (2005) used harmonic radar to track recruited flight paths.

Recent experiments also show social learning affects accurate dance performance: bees deprived of experienced dancers before their first dance make larger directional/disorder errors and persistent distance errors.

- `STRUCTURE`: continuous geometry/timing.
- `REFERENCE_LIKE`: strong displaced resource-location communication.
- `RECEIVER BEHAVIOR`: strong recruitment/navigation evidence.
- `SOCIAL LEARNING`: contributes to correct signal production.
- `SEMANTIC CLAIM_CEILING`: spatially referential recruitment code grounded in bee navigation/perceptual mechanisms.

**Critical UNVTRSLR lesson:** even when two organisms interact in the same physical world, the communicated variable may be a species-specific perceptual quantity. `distance` in the evaluator's Euclidean meters is not automatically the sender's or receiver's semantic quantity.

References:
- Esch, H. E., Zhang, S., Srinivasan, M. V. & Tautz, J. (2001). *Honeybee dances communicate distances measured by optic flow*. Nature 411:581-583. DOI `10.1038/35079072`.
- Riley, J. R. et al. (2005). *The flight paths of honeybees recruited by the waggle dance*. Nature 435:205-207. DOI `10.1038/nature03526`.
- Dong, S. et al. (2023). social learning of waggle-dance signaling. Science. DOI `10.1126/science.ade1702`.

## 12. Weakly electric fish: the communication channel can also be the sensory medium

Weakly electric fish produce electric organ discharges used both to sense the environment and to communicate socially. Brief frequency/amplitude modulations (`chirps`) vary with courtship/aggression and social context.

- `CHANNEL`: electric field rather than acoustic/visual signal.
- `DUAL USE`: electrolocation and electrocommunication share the same physical medium.
- `STRUCTURE/FUNCTION`: social signals have characteristic modulations and receiver processing.
- `SEMANTIC CLAIM_CEILING`: context-linked social electric signaling; no evidence for human-like lexical semantics.

UNVTRSLR consequence: `sensor stream` and `message channel` need not be separable. A candidate signal may literally be a modulation of the medium an organism uses to perceive its world.

Reference: Dunlap, K. D. et al. (2021). *Vocal and Electric Fish: Revisiting a Comparison of Two Teleost Models in the Neuroethology of Social Behavior*. Frontiers in Neural Circuits 15:706148. DOI `10.3389/fncir.2021.706148`.

## 13. Cephalopod dynamic skin patterning: the same actuator supports camouflage, social signaling, deception, and defense

Cuttlefish and other cephalopods dynamically change skin color/contrast, texture, posture, and motion. Ethological/neurobiological work shows these patterns serve both environmental camouflage and social functions such as courtship, dominance/agonism, and mating behavior. Some polarization patterns are invisible to human vision.

- `STRUCTURE`: rich spatial/temporal body patterns.
- `FUNCTION`: both communicative and noncommunicative functions depending on context.
- `MULTIMODAL`: chromatic, textural, postural, motion, and polarization dimensions.
- `SEMANTIC CLAIM_CEILING`: structured visual signaling with demonstrated social functions; detailed semantics of most patterns remain limited.

UNVTRSLR consequence: **signalhood is context-dependent even for the same physical pattern generator**. A system that classifies every high-contrast skin pattern as communication will hallucinate messages from camouflage/defense.

References:
- Crook, A. C., Baddeley, R. & Osorio, D. (2002). *Identifying the structure in cuttlefish visual signals*. Philosophical Transactions/Royal Society B. DOI associated with the published article.
- How, M. J. et al. (2017). *Dynamic Skin Patterns in Cephalopods*. Frontiers in Physiology 8:393. DOI `10.3389/fphys.2017.00393`.

## 14. Ant pheromone trails: communication can be persistent, many-to-many, and partially detached from a current sender

Ant recruitment often uses chemical trails. A scout can deposit pheromone while returning from food; nestmates later follow the chemical gradient. Trail strength and reinforcement can relate to recruitment dynamics and resource context, while chemicals decay over time.

- `CHANNEL`: diffusing/persistent chemical field.
- `TEMPORAL STRUCTURE`: signal can outlast the emitting individual at a location.
- `NETWORK`: many senders can reinforce; many receivers can sample.
- `SEMANTIC CLAIM_CEILING`: chemical recruitment/collective coordination, not evidence for symbolic language.

UNVTRSLR consequence: the generic theory should not require a one-sender/one-receiver message event. An environmental state can become an asynchronously sampled communication substrate. Provenance may be collective and lossy.

References:
- Hölldobler, B. (1995). *The chemistry of social regulation: multicomponent signals in ant societies*. PNAS 92(1):19-22. DOI `10.1073/pnas.92.1.19`.
- Middleton, E. J. T. & Latty, T. (2020). ant foraging/recruitment review in *Insects* 11:268, including trail and tandem recruitment mechanisms.

## 15. Substrate-borne vibration: communication does not require a freely propagating acoustic or visual channel

Arthropods and other animals communicate through vibrations traveling through leaves, webs, soil, or other substrates. Documented functions include courtship, mate recognition, competition, danger signaling, foraging, and parental/social interactions.

- `CHANNEL`: surface-borne mechanical waves.
- `BOUNDARY PROBLEM`: what counts as signal depends on properties of the substrate and receiver mechanosensation.
- `SEMANTIC CLAIM_CEILING`: established signal functions in many taxa; system-specific content varies widely.

UNVTRSLR consequence: physical-layer discovery must precede tokenization. A receiver optimized only for air pressure/light may literally miss the communication surface.

Reference: Cocroft, R. B. and related biotremology literature; see 2020 review *Biotremology in arthropods* (Learning & Behavior 48), DOI `10.3758/s13420-020-00428-3`.

# Cross-species synthesis

## 16. The weakest defensible common denominator is not `word`, `message`, or even discrete sender/receiver turn

Across the reviewed systems, no common evidence supports a universal requirement for:

- speech or sound;
- visual gesture;
- discrete symbols;
- one-dimensional sequences;
- explicit object reference;
- intentional signal production;
- a one-sender/one-receiver exchange;
- immediate presence of the sender;
- a separate channel versus sensorium;
- or a shared human physical quantity such as meters of distance.

A weaker cross-species formulation is:

> **A communication candidate is a reproducible modification of a medium or shared state whose production/history is coupled to one system and whose availability causally or predictively constrains another system's behavior or internal state beyond an appropriate noncommunicative null.**

This is intentionally below semantics. Semantic grounding requires additional invariance, intervention, transfer, or reference/function evidence appropriate to the system.

**Evidence class:** `INFERENCE / PROJECT_HYPOTHESIS`.

## 17. `Two agents with a shared observable environment` is useful experimentally but too narrow as the general theory

Honeybee dances fit a dyadic sender/receiver abstraction reasonably well. Ant chemical trails do not: the signal may be collectively reinforced and persist in the environment. Electric fish blur signal and sensing. Sperm whales can chorus with alternation and overlap. Cephalopods use the same body-pattern machinery for communication and noncommunication.

Recommended theory-level wording:

> **two or more coupled adaptive systems with partially overlapping observables and influence surfaces**

The original two-agent/shared-world case remains the first controlled engineering experiment because it is tractable and falsifiable.

## 18. `Signalhood` should be a multi-hypothesis model, not a binary classifier

A physical pattern may be:

- communicative;
- sensory/probing behavior;
- environmental side effect;
- self-regulation;
- camouflage;
- defensive display;
- accidental correlation;
- a mixed-use behavior whose function depends on audience/context.

Cephalopods and electric fish make this especially obvious.

Recommended evidence record:

`pattern -> context -> audience/receiver -> causal response -> alternative function hypotheses -> confidence`.

## 19. Reference is not one thing

Nonhuman evidence separates several phenomena often collapsed into `reference`:

- external-stimulus-specific alarm information (vervets);
- displaced resource-vector communication (honeybees);
- individual addressing through a learned self-signature copied by others (dolphins);
- individually specific nonimitative addressing (elephants);
- caller/unit/clan identity cues (sperm whales);
- call spectra predictive of addressee/context (bats).

UNVTRSLR should represent these as different tested relations rather than projecting one universal `REFERENT` type.

## 20. Combination is not automatically compositional semantics

Sperm-whale codas have rich combinatorial acoustic structure while semantics remain largely unknown. Japanese great-tit playback supports bounded composition-like interpretation. Campbell's-monkey suffixation is a strong candidate but still debated in exact analogy.

Therefore separate:

`FORM_COMBINATION`
`RECEIVER_COMBINATION_SENSITIVITY`
`PREDICTABLE_FUNCTION_COMBINATION`
`COMPOSITIONAL_SEMANTIC_GENERALIZATION`

A model may pass the first two and fail the latter two.

## 21. Intentionality is useful evidence where available, not a universal semantic gate

Great ape gesture provides strong behavioral evidence of audience sensitivity, persistence, and elaboration. Honeybee dance and ant trails demonstrate sophisticated communication without the same intentionality evidence.

UNVTRSLR should use intentionality/audience criteria to strengthen a signalhood hypothesis where supported, but should not require a human-like theory of mind before recognizing communication.

## 22. Social learning and culture can shape signal systems far outside humans

Evidence includes learned dolphin signature whistles, sperm-whale vocal clans, socially shaped honeybee dance accuracy, parrots' vocal imitation, birdsong culture, and other systems.

UNVTRSLR consequence: `innate versus learned` should not be a binary semantic distinction. Bridge provenance should record individual learning, social transmission, population convention, and stable biological constraints separately.

## 23. Perceptual relativity is a first-class grounding problem

Honeybee dance is the clearest warning: what a human calls `distance` is mediated by bee optic-flow odometry. Electric fish experience social/sensory structure in an electric field. Cephalopods may use polarization cues invisible to humans.

A shared physical world does not imply shared observables or shared natural variables.

R0.5 should therefore distinguish:

- evaluator physical variable;
- sender-available perceptual variable;
- receiver-available perceptual variable;
- learned cross-system invariant;
- mediator-computed normalization.

## 24. Nonhuman data weaken several `first 100` items as universal primitives

The strongest anthropocentric families are currently:

- persistent individual/object identity as default segmentation;
- actor/action/patient roles;
- possession/access;
- self/other as a discrete symbol;
- goal and belief representation;
- assertion/request/question/warning as universal speech-act families;
- exact counterfactual report;
- one-to-one referential semantics.

These remain valuable **human-semantic challenge families**, but the generic qualification program should allow a system to bypass or return `NOT_APPLICABLE_TO_OBSERVED_COMMUNICATION_SYSTEM` rather than forcing them into every communicator's ontology.

## 25. Strongest revised thesis

After human verbal, human signed/nonverbal, and nonhuman evidence, the defensible target becomes:

> Given two or more initially unaligned adaptive systems with some physically available coupling, can a mediator discover which patterns function communicatively, determine which distinctions are identifiable and attributable to participant interaction rather than mediation subsidy, stabilize or recover grounded conventions, and recursively expand the bridge — while preserving uncertainty, alternative functions, non-equivalence, and `NOT_APPLICABLE` outcomes?

The **first 100** then become an adversarial benchmark of semantic capability, not a presumed universal curriculum.

The original extraterrestrial ambition survives, but only as the far end of a ladder whose early gates are signal-surface discovery, identifiability, attribution, and scoped function/reference testing.
