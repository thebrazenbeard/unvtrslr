# Nonhuman Communication Source Notes

Status: `PASS_3_REFERENCE_SUPPLEMENT`

This file records the principal empirical/review sources used by `research/NONHUMAN_COMMUNICATION.md`. Terminology such as `semantic`, `syntax`, `name`, or `language` is retained when it is part of a source title or source-specific literature, but UNVTRSLR applies the narrower evidence ceilings documented in the research review.

## Primates — vocal and gestural

### Seyfarth, R. M., Cheney, D. L. & Marler, P. (1980). “Vervet monkey alarm calls: Semantic communication in a free-ranging primate.” *Animal Behaviour* 28(4), 1070-1094.
DOI: `10.1016/S0003-3472(80)80097-2`

**Use:** classic production/playback evidence for predator-linked alarm categories and receiver responses in the absence of the predator.

### Price, T. et al. (2015). “Vervets revisited: A quantitative analysis of alarm call structure and context specificity.” *Scientific Reports* 5:13220.
DOI: `10.1038/srep13220`

**Use:** quantitative warning against oversimplified one-call/one-context lexicalization.

### Ouattara, K., Lemasson, A. & Zuberbühler, K. Campbell's-monkey call-combination/suffix studies (2009-2010), including “Campbell's monkeys concatenate vocalizations into context-specific call sequences.”

**Use:** strong comparative case for systematic meaning-modifying call combination; exact human morphology/syntax analogy remains bounded.

### Suzuki, T. N., Wheatcroft, D. & Griesser, M. (2020). “The syntax-semantics interface in animal vocal communication.” *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 375:20180405.
DOI: `10.1098/rstb.2018.0405`

**Use:** comparative review distinguishing structural combination from evidence for compositional semantics.

### Takahashi, D. Y., Narayanan, D. Z. & Ghazanfar, A. A. (2013). “Coupled Oscillator Dynamics of Vocal Turn-Taking in Monkeys.” *Current Biology* 23, 2162-2168.
DOI: `10.1016/j.cub.2013.09.005`

### Takahashi, D. Y. et al. (2016). “Early development of turn-taking with parents shapes vocal acoustics in infant marmoset monkeys.” *Philosophical Transactions B* 371:20150370.
DOI: `10.1098/rstb.2015.0370`

### “A model of marmoset monkey vocal turn-taking” (2024), Royal Society publication.

**Use:** larger-sample modeling preserves overlap avoidance/turn-like interaction while challenging a broad coupled-oscillator mechanism. Important example of `behavioral regularity != correct explanatory model`.

### Hobaiter, C. & Byrne, R. W. (2014). “The Meanings of Chimpanzee Gestures.” *Current Biology* 24(14), 1596-1600.
DOI: `10.1016/j.cub.2014.05.066`

### Byrne, R. W. et al. (2017). “Great ape gestures: intentional communication with a rich set of innate signals.” *Animal Cognition* 20, 755-769.
DOI: `10.1007/s10071-017-1096-4`

**Use:** operational intentionality evidence via audience targeting, response waiting, persistence, elaboration, and context-sensitive gesture use.

## Cetaceans

### King, S. L. & Janik, V. M. (2013). “Bottlenose dolphins can use learned vocal labels to address each other.” *PNAS* 110(32), 13216-13221.
DOI: `10.1073/pnas.1304459110`

**Use:** synthesized signature-whistle playback supports learned individual-address labels. `Name` remains analogy rather than human-equivalent ontology.

### Gero, S., Whitehead, H. & Rendell, L. (2016). “Individual, unit and vocal clan level identity cues in sperm whale codas.” *Royal Society Open Science* 3:150372.
DOI: `10.1098/rsos.150372`

**Use:** identity information and culturally transmitted vocal-clan structure.

### Sharma, P. et al. (2024). “Contextual and combinatorial structure in sperm whale vocalisations.” *Nature Communications* 15:3617.
DOI: `10.1038/s41467-024-47221-8`

**Use:** rhythm, tempo, rubato, ornamentation, interactional context, and combinatorial acoustic organization. The article explicitly states that it does not characterize call semantics and lacks playback semantic tests.

## Birds

### Suzuki, T. N., Wheatcroft, D. & Griesser, M. (2016). “Experimental evidence for compositional syntax in bird calls.” *Nature Communications* 7:10986.
DOI: `10.1038/ncomms10986`

**Use:** playback-based evidence that Japanese great tits respond to ordered call combinations in ways consistent with bounded compositional interpretation.

### Balsby, T. J. S., Momberg, J. V. & Dabelsteen, T. (2012). “Vocal Imitation in Parrots Allows Addressing of Specific Individuals in a Dynamic Communication Network.” *PLOS ONE* 7:e49747.
DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0049747`

**Use:** playback evidence for individual addressing through copied contact-call structure rather than an independently arbitrary label.

### Bruno, J. H., Jarvis, E. D., Liberman, M. & Tchernichovski, O. (2021). “Birdsong Learning and Culture: Analogies with Human Spoken Language.” *Annual Review of Linguistics* 7, 449-472.
DOI: `10.1146/annurev-linguistics-090420-121034`

**Use:** review of vocal learning and cultural transmission in songbirds; supports learned/cultural signal variation without equating birdsong with human semantics.

## Elephants and bats

### Pardo, M. A. et al. (2024). “African elephants address one another with individually specific name-like calls.” *Nature Ecology & Evolution*.
DOI: `10.1038/s41559-024-02420-w`

**Use:** machine-learning plus field-playback evidence for receiver-specific, name-like addressing that is not explained by simple imitation of receiver calls. Use `name-like`, not `human names`.

### Prat, Y., Taub, M. & Yovel, Y. (2016). “Everyday bat vocalizations contain information about emitter, addressee, context, and behavior.” *Scientific Reports* 6:39419.
DOI: `10.1038/srep39419`

**Use:** large-scale audio/video evidence that bat vocalizations contain classifier-readable information about multiple social variables. Does not by itself establish semantic representations.

## Honeybees

### Esch, H. E., Zhang, S., Srinivasan, M. V. & Tautz, J. (2001). “Honeybee dances communicate distances measured by optic flow.” *Nature* 411, 581-583.
DOI: `10.1038/35079072`

**Use:** demonstrates that communicated route-distance information tracks a bee-specific optic-flow odometer rather than simple human absolute meters.

### Riley, J. R. et al. (2005). “The flight paths of honeybees recruited by the waggle dance.” *Nature* 435, 205-207.
DOI: `10.1038/nature03526`

**Use:** harmonic-radar evidence that recruits use dance information to navigate toward indicated resource locations.

### Dong, S. et al. (2023). honeybee waggle-dance social-learning study, *Science*.
DOI: `10.1126/science.ade1702`

**Use:** bees deprived of experienced dance-following before their first dances make larger directional/disorder errors and persistent distance-encoding errors. Supports social learning in signal production.

## Electric communication

### Dunlap, K. D., Koukos, D., Chagnaud, B. P., Zakon, H. H. & Bass, A. H. (2021). “Vocal and Electric Fish: Revisiting a Comparison of Two Teleost Models in the Neuroethology of Social Behavior.” *Frontiers in Neural Circuits* 15:706148.
DOI: `10.3389/fncir.2021.706148`

**Use:** electric organ discharges serve electrolocation and social electrocommunication; transient chirps vary with social context. Direct challenge to a clean sensor/channel boundary.

## Cephalopods

### Crook, A. C., Baddeley, R. & Osorio, D. (2002). “Identifying the structure in cuttlefish visual signals.” Royal Society publication.

**Use:** quantitative analysis of complex cuttlefish body-pattern repertoires; signal structure alone does not determine communicative function.

### How, M. J. et al. (2017). “Dynamic Skin Patterns in Cephalopods.” *Frontiers in Physiology* 8:393.
DOI: `10.3389/fphys.2017.00393`

**Use:** dynamic visual patterning used across signaling, camouflage, courtship, and other contexts; strong unknown-signalhood stress test.

## Chemical and substrate-borne communication

### Hölldobler, B. (1995). “The chemistry of social regulation: multicomponent signals in ant societies.” *PNAS* 92(1), 19-22.
DOI: `10.1073/pnas.92.1.19`

**Use:** multicomponent chemical signals and collective social regulation; supports persistent/field-like, many-to-many communication representations.

### Middleton, E. J. T. & Latty, T. (2020). review of ant foraging recruitment and information use.

**Use:** trail pheromone and tandem-run recruitment variants; shows communication can be persistent environmental modification rather than a discrete message event.

### “Biotremology in arthropods” (2020). *Learning & Behavior* 48.
DOI: `10.3758/s13420-020-00428-3`

**Use:** review of substrate-borne vibrational communication in arthropods across courtship, recognition, competition, danger, foraging, and social behavior.

## Claim ceiling

Together these sources establish rich, diverse nonhuman communication functions across acoustic, visual, motion, chemical, electrical, tactile/substrate, and environmental channels. They do not establish that any reviewed system is equivalent to human language, that acoustic/behavioral structure is automatically semantic, or that a fixed cross-species ontology exists.
