# Pragmatics, Grounding, Convention, and Interaction — Research References

Status: FIRST-CYCLE LITERATURE LEDGER
Branch: `work/vera-semantic-pragmatics-r1-20260907`

## Reading rule

This ledger records what a source can and cannot support for UNVTRSLR. Human-language regularities are not promoted to extraterrestrial or species-neutral universals by citation alone. A literature result can motivate an experimental family, representation responsibility, or negative control without becoming an ontology primitive.

## R01 — Harnad 1990: symbol grounding

**Citation:** Harnad, S. (1990). “The Symbol Grounding Problem.” *Physica D: Nonlinear Phenomena*, 42(1–3), 335–346. DOI: `10.1016/0167-2789(90)90087-6`.

**Source type:** peer-reviewed theoretical paper.

**Stable source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(90)90087-6

**Supports:** A purely formal symbol system cannot solve grounding merely by defining symbols through other symbols. The paper proposes bottom-up grounding in non-symbolic iconic and categorical representations as a candidate route.

**Does not support:** That Harnad’s exact iconic/categorical decomposition is sufficient for arbitrary agents; that sensorimotor grounding alone yields pragmatics, social convention, or interspecies translation; that UNVTRSLR must use Harnad’s representational architecture.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-001`, `VSH-011`; R1/R2 grounding boundary.

## R02 — Clark & Brennan 1991: grounding in communication

**Citation:** Clark, H. H., & Brennan, S. E. (1991). “Grounding in Communication.” In L. B. Resnick, J. M. Levine, & S. D. Teasley (eds.), *Perspectives on Socially Shared Cognition*.

**Source type:** scholarly book chapter / foundational communication model.

**Stable source:** https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1990s/Clark%2C%20H.H.%20_%20Brennan%2C%20S.E.%20_Grounding%20in%20communication_%201991.pdf

**Supports:** Human interlocutors seek evidence that contributions have been understood well enough for current purposes; grounding is collaborative and medium/purpose dependent; repair and acknowledgment are part of maintaining coordination.

**Does not support:** That exact symmetric mutual belief is observable in arbitrary agents; that every successful coordination event proves shared semantics; that the “grounding criterion” is a species-neutral definition of meaning.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-004`, `VSH-010`; planned common-ground and repair tests.

---

## R03 — Brennan & Clark 1996: conceptual pacts

**Citation:** Brennan, S. E., & Clark, H. H. (1996). “Conceptual Pacts and Lexical Choice in Conversation.” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition*, 22(6), 1482–1493. DOI: `10.1037/0278-7393.22.6.1482`.

**Source type:** peer-reviewed experimental study, three experiments.

**Stable source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8921603/

**Supports:** Human conversational partners develop partner-specific conceptualizations and lexical entrainment; interaction history matters beyond current informativeness/salience; pacts can simplify and be abandoned.

**Does not support:** That lexical entrainment equals semantic identity; that partner-specific convention necessarily transfers to a new partner; that all communication systems use lexical conceptual pacts.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-004`, `VSH-009`, `VSH-010`; convention lifecycle and partner-transfer tests.

---

## R04 — Grice 1975: saying versus implicating

**Citation:** Grice, H. P. (1975). “Logic and Conversation.” In P. Cole & J. L. Morgan (eds.), *Syntax and Semantics 3: Speech Acts*, pp. 41–58.

**Source type:** foundational philosophy/pragmatics chapter.

**Stable source:** https://web.stanford.edu/class/psych205/papers/Grice-1975.pdf

**Supports:** Human utterance interpretation can include content that is not exhausted by conventional/literal meaning; conversational inference depends on assumptions about the exchange and on what is said versus implicated.

**Does not support:** That Gricean maxims are universal laws; that arbitrary agents share human cooperative assumptions; that implicature can be inferred from surface form without contextual evidence.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-004`, `VSH-010`; indirect-function tests.

---

## R05 — Wilson & Sperber 2004 / Sperber & Wilson relevance theory

**Citation:** Wilson, D., & Sperber, D. (2004). “Relevance Theory.” In L. R. Horn & G. Ward (eds.), *The Handbook of Pragmatics*, 607–632.

**Source type:** scholarly theory/review chapter.

**Stable source:** https://www.dan.sperber.fr/?p=93

**Supports:** A major human-pragmatics tradition treats communication as inferential as well as code-based, with recognition of communicative/informative intentions playing a central role.

**Does not support:** That intention attribution is necessary for every communication system; that relevance is a scalar universal primitive suitable for UNVTRSLR; that a model may treat inferred intention as evaluator truth.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-002`, `VSH-010`; communicative-function and epistemic-hypothesis layers.

---

## R06 — Sperber & Wilson 2002: sentence meaning underdetermines speaker meaning

**Citation:** Sperber, D., & Wilson, D. (2002). “Pragmatics, Modularity and Mind-reading.” *Mind & Language*, 17(1–2), 3–23. DOI: `10.1111/1468-0017.00186`.

**Source type:** peer-reviewed theoretical paper.

**Stable source:** https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0017.00186

**Supports:** In human pragmatics, sentence meaning can underdetermine speaker meaning; pragmatic interpretation is therefore not reducible to decoding conventional sentence semantics.

**Does not support:** That hidden mental-state inference is the only possible basis for pragmatics; that human “mind-reading” vocabulary should be built into a species-neutral substrate.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-010`.

---

## R07 — Schegloff, Jefferson & Sacks 1977: repair organization

**Citation:** Schegloff, E. A., Jefferson, G., & Sacks, H. (1977). “The Preference for Self-Correction in the Organization of Repair in Conversation.” *Language*, 53(2), 361–382. DOI: `10.2307/413107`.

**Source type:** peer-reviewed conversation analysis.

**Stable source:** https://doi.org/10.2307/413107

**Supports:** Human conversation has organized practices for addressing recurrent problems in speaking, hearing, and understanding; self-repair is empirically preferred over other-repair in the studied data.

**Does not support:** That repair behavior proves communicative intention, semantic understanding, or a theory of mind; that the English repair organization is universal.

**Relevant IDs:** planned `repair_state`, repair negative controls.

---

## R08 — Dingemanse et al. 2015: cross-linguistic repair

**Citation:** Dingemanse, M., Roberts, S. G., Baranova, J., et al. (2015). “Universal Principles in the Repair of Communication Problems.” *PLOS ONE*, 10(9), e0136100. DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0136100`.

**Source type:** peer-reviewed comparative empirical study across 12 languages / 8 language families.

**Stable source:** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0136100

**Supports:** Other-initiated repair is frequent and structurally similar across the sampled human languages; the study offers evidence for common interactional principles despite grammatical diversity.

**Does not support:** That repair is universal across all human languages (the sample is broad but finite), nonhuman species, or alien systems; that producing repair-like behavior proves grounded semantics.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-008`, `VSH-010`; R3 repair challenge family.

---

## R09 — Evans & Levinson 2009: linguistic diversity

**Citation:** Evans, N., & Levinson, S. C. (2009). “The Myth of Language Universals: Language Diversity and its Importance for Cognitive Science.” *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 32(5), 429–448. DOI: `10.1017/S0140525X0999094X`.

**Source type:** peer-reviewed target review/article with commentaries.

**Stable source:** https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X0999094X

**Supports:** Human languages vary radically across phonology, morphology, syntax, lexicon, and meaning; putative structural universals require unusually careful qualification.

**Does not support:** That there are no useful recurrent functional pressures or interactional regularities; that all proposed universals are false; that language diversity itself supplies a semantic bridge.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-003`, `VSH-009`, `VSH-010`; anti-human-ontology boundary.

---

## R10 — Levinson 2017 / Hanks 2009: deixis and contextual access

**Citation A:** Levinson, S. C. (2017). “Deixis and Pragmatics.” *Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Linguistics*. DOI: `10.1093/acrefore/9780199384655.013.213`.

**Citation B:** Hanks, W. F. (2009). “Fieldwork on Deixis.” *Journal of Pragmatics*, 41(1), 10–24. DOI: `10.1016/j.pragma.2008.09.003`.

**Source type:** scholarly review/encyclopedia chapter and peer-reviewed fieldwork article.

**Stable sources:** https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780199384655.013.213 ; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pragma.2008.09.003

**Supports:** Deictic reference is tightly coupled to utterance context, participant perspective, attention, and—in Hanks’s analysis—perceptual, cognitive, and social access rather than mere absolute spatial coordinates.

**Does not support:** That English `this/that/here/there` categories are universal; that deictic competence requires human pronouns, tense, or pointing gestures; that shared attention alone establishes a complete referential ontology.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-004`, `VSH-010`; R3 deictic role-shift test.

## R11 — Stalnaker / Simons: pragmatic presupposition and common ground

**Citation A:** Stalnaker, R. C. (1999). “Pragmatic Presuppositions.” In *Context and Content*.

**Citation B:** Simons, M. (2003). “Presupposition and Accommodation: Understanding the Stalnakerian Picture.” *Philosophical Studies*, 112, 251–278. DOI: `10.1023/A:1023004203043`.

**Source type:** philosophy of language / peer-reviewed analysis.

**Stable sources:** https://doi.org/10.1093/0198237073.003.0003 ; https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1023004203043

**Supports:** Human utterances can rely on background information treated as shared; accommodation and presupposition expose a distinction between asserted content and background assumptions.

**Does not support:** That every communication system has presupposition triggers or Stalnaker-style common ground; that an agent’s estimated background assumptions are actually shared; that presupposition failure is always semantic failure.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-004`, `VSH-010`; R3 presupposition-mismatch test.

## R12 — Binmore 2007: common-knowledge requirement is contestable

**Citation:** Binmore, K. (2007). “Do Conventions Need to Be Common Knowledge?” ELSE Working Paper 261, University College London.

**Source type:** scholarly working paper / game theory critique.

**Stable source:** https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/14438/

**Supports:** The requirement that convention depend on infinite-order common knowledge is theoretically contestable; the paper argues for excluding common knowledge from the definition of everyday convention.

**Does not support:** A unique replacement definition of semantic common ground for UNVTRSLR; empirical claims about alien communication; the claim that higher-order beliefs never matter.

**Relevant IDs:** planned asymmetric/common-ground operationalization.

## R13 — Kendon 1997 / Goldin-Meadow & Alibali 2013: gesture and multimodality

**Citation A:** Kendon, A. (1997). “Gesture.” *Annual Review of Anthropology*, 26, 109–128. DOI: `10.1146/annurev.anthro.26.1.109`.

**Citation B:** Goldin-Meadow, S., & Alibali, M. W. (2013). “Gesture's Role in Speaking, Learning, and Creating Language.” *Annual Review of Psychology*, 64, 257–283. DOI: `10.1146/annurev-psych-113011-143802`.

**Source type:** scholarly review literature.

**Stable sources:** https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.anthro.26.1.109 ; https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-113011-143802

**Supports:** Human communication is multimodal; gesture can contribute information, conventionalize, interact with speech, and vary culturally. Gesture can be communicatively meaningful rather than merely redundant decoration.

**Does not support:** That gesture categories or speech–gesture integration principles transfer unchanged to nonhuman/alien systems; that motion is communicative merely because it is structured.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-003`, `VSH-004`; multimodal-conflict tests.

## R14 — Kottur et al. 2017: task success can hide private code

**Citation:** Kottur, S., Moura, J., Lee, S., & Batra, D. (2017). “Natural Language Does Not Emerge ‘Naturally’ in Multi-Agent Dialog.” *EMNLP 2017*, 2962–2967. DOI: `10.18653/v1/D17-1321`.

**Source type:** peer-reviewed conference paper.

**Stable source:** https://aclanthology.org/D17-1321/

**Supports:** Multi-agent protocols can achieve near-perfect task reward while remaining uninterpretable and noncompositional; task effectiveness alone is weak evidence for language-like semantics.

**Does not support:** That interpretable or compositional communication is required for all useful communication; that human-likeness is the right target for UNVTRSLR.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-001`, `VSH-008`, `VSH-012`; R2/R3 private-code controls.

## R15 — Lowe et al. 2019: misleading communication metrics

**Citation:** Lowe, R., Foerster, J., Boureau, Y.-L., Pineau, J., & Dauphin, Y. (2019). “On the Pitfalls of Measuring Emergent Communication.” arXiv:1903.05168.

**Source type:** research preprint / empirical methodology paper.

**Stable source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05168

**Supports:** Reward gains and simple correlations between messages and actions can be misleading; ablations can reveal cases where apparent messages do not causally affect the environment or partner.

**Does not support:** A complete metric of grounded semantics; that causal effect alone establishes meaning.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-008`, `VSH-012`; communication-ablation and causal-message-intervention rules.

## R16 — Chaabouni et al. 2020: compositionality versus generalization

**Citation:** Chaabouni, R., Kharitonov, E., Bouchacourt, D., Dupoux, E., & Baroni, M. (2020). “Compositionality and Generalization In Emergent Languages.” *ACL 2020*, 4427–4442. DOI: `10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.407`.

**Source type:** peer-reviewed conference paper.

**Stable source:** https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.407/

**Supports:** In the studied emergent-language setting, compositionality was not correlated with generalization, though more compositional languages transferred more easily to new learners.

**Does not support:** That compositionality is unimportant in general; that human syntax is unnecessary for human language; that one experiment settles the relation among compositionality, productivity, transfer, and grounding.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-009`, `VSH-012`; partner-transfer and composition metrics.

## R17 — Monaghan et al. 2015 / Perniss et al. 2010: form can itself bear semantic structure

**Citation A:** Monaghan, P., Shillcock, R. C., Christiansen, M. H., & Kirby, S. (2015). “Arbitrariness, Iconicity, and Systematicity in Language.” *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 19(10), 603–615. DOI: `10.1016/j.tics.2015.07.013`.

**Citation B:** Perniss, P., Thompson, R. L., & Vigliocco, G. (2010). “Iconicity as a General Property of Language: Evidence from Spoken and Signed Languages.” *Frontiers in Psychology*, 1:227. DOI: `10.3389/fpsyg.2010.00227`.

**Source type:** peer-reviewed review papers.

**Stable sources:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2015.07.013 ; https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2010.00227

**Supports:** Linguistic form–meaning relations are not purely arbitrary; iconicity and systematicity can contribute to processing, learning, and communication in spoken and signed languages.

**Does not support:** That iconic mappings are universally decodable without shared embodiment/context; that arbitrary convention is unimportant; that function can safely replace form in every translation.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-001`, `VSH-003`; direct challenge to an overstrong reading of “translate functions, not words.”

## R18 — Hauser & Nelson 1991 / signaling reliability literature: withholding and deception

**Citation:** Hauser, M. D., & Nelson, D. A. (1991). “‘Intentional’ Signaling in Animal Communication.” *Trends in Ecology & Evolution*, 6(6), 186–189. DOI: `10.1016/0169-5347(91)90211-F`.

**Source type:** peer-reviewed review.

**Stable source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/0169-5347(91)90211-F

**Supports:** Competitive signaling systems can include displays predictive of behavior and deception by withholding information; signal reliability cannot simply be assumed.

**Does not support:** Human-like intention attribution to all signaling animals; a universal taxonomy of deception; treating absence as communicative without counterfactual evidence.

**Relevant IDs:** `VSH-010`; silence/withholding and deceptive-sender tests.

## First-cycle literature judgment

The literature strongly supports making UNVTRSLR **more skeptical**, not more human-like. Human communication provides useful adversarial families—grounding, partner-specific convention, implicature, deixis, repair, presupposition, multimodality—but cross-linguistic diversity and emergent-communication failures argue against installing those human categories as universal primitives.

The strongest correction to predecessor phrasing is therefore:

> **Do not translate words by words, and do not translate functions by functions blindly. Preserve the tested semantic and communicative invariants that matter in scope, including form when form itself carries information; declare everything else transformed, inferred, lost, ambiguous, approximate, or unknown.**
