The ordering is an audit aid rather than a claim that all species traverse these stages. Social learning/culture and intentionality may be orthogonal to the strongest semantic evidence available in a given system.

## Strongest cross-species architecture changes

1. `two agents exchanging messages in a shared environment` remains a good first experiment but is too narrow for the general theory.
2. The generalized candidate subject is `two or more initially unaligned adaptive systems or coupled processes with partially overlapping observables and influence surfaces`.
3. `sender`, `receiver`, `message`, `channel`, `object`, `agent`, and `signal` may be learned hypotheses rather than universal primitives.
4. A shared physical world does not guarantee shared semantic variables. Honeybee distance communication demonstrates that organism-specific perceptual metrics can mediate a physical quantity.
5. Communication can be persistent, collectively authored, asynchronous, and sampled after the original producer is gone.
6. Communication and environmental sensing may share the same physical substrate, as in electrocommunication/electrolocation.
7. The same observable actuator/pattern generator can be communicative in one context and noncommunicative in another, so signalhood must remain contextual.
8. Rich acoustic or sequential structure must remain below semantic qualification unless receiver/world evidence supports a stronger claim.
9. Individual addressing is a relation, not proof of a universal `NAME` primitive; different species implement addressing through materially different mechanisms.
10. Intentionality evidence is highly informative where operationally demonstrated but cannot be required for all communication.
11. The first 100 survive as an adversarial challenge inventory, not a universal concept list or fixed developmental ladder.
12. The project now permits `NOT_APPLICABLE_TO_OBSERVED_COMMUNICATION_SYSTEM` when a challenge presupposes unsupported ontology rather than treating absence as communication failure.

## Cross-pass thesis refinement

The stronger current project thesis is:

> Under declared conditions where an interaction surface contains recoverable distinguishing structure and no privileged mediator supplies the target correspondence, initially unaligned systems may be able to construct scoped, testable, reusable cross-system semantic correspondences without a pre-shared symbolic language.

Literal zero-semantic self-description is not accepted as the first primitive. The surviving staged hypothesis remains:

`self-demonstrating -> convention-forming -> recursively self-describing`

## Identifiability and attribution split

The cross-pass synthesis preserves two separate gates:

- `DISTINCTION_IDENTIFIABLE_IN_SURFACE`
- `SEMANTIC_BRIDGE_ATTRIBUTABLE_TO_LEARNER`

A privileged renderer, adapter, evaluator, or mediator can make a distinction learner-visible while already doing the semantic normalization. Such success does not establish learner bridge construction.

## Claim ceiling

Pass 3 supports a broader empirical claim that communication can be:

- vocal or non-vocal;
- discrete or continuous;
- sequential or simultaneous;
- transient or persistent;
- dyadic or collectively authored;
- dedicated-channel or sensor/channel-overlapping;
- individually learned or culturally/socially transmitted;
- strongly intentional under operational criteria in some systems and not demonstrably so in others.

It does **not** establish:

- that animal communication is human language;
- that all communication systems possess a lexicon;
- that all systems are semantically translatable into human categories;
- that intentionality is universal;
- that composition is universal;
- that a shared world solves perceptual non-isomorphism;
- that one universal ontology exists;
- that the first 100 are natural semantic kinds;
- that a self-describing interstellar protocol is feasible;
- or that UNVTRSLR has achieved semantic qualification.
